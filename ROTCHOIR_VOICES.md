# The Rot Choir — Five Voices

> A lore reference for the five named NPCs introduced into the Rot Choir
> faction: **Solm, Vurn, Amai, Isk, Ketch.** Each is a former individual
> whom the Choir absorbed; each retains the cadence of the lost culture
> that raised them. The Choir is one — and yet these five remain audibly
> themselves within it.
>
> **Phase 11 (C8.5) compliance rewrite.** The original five carried
> real-language personal names with explicit living-tradition labels,
> against the Bible §IX naming rule. Player-facing names and voice specs
> are now constructed; the engine-facing blueprint/conversation IDs are
> frozen for save-compat (see the table at the bottom and
> `Lore/TERMS.md`). The original inspirations are credited in the
> design-note ledger at the end, where they belong.

---

## The conceit

The Rot Choir is a single mycelial collective. Its `ChoirTendril_1` voice
speaks in the plural — *"we taste you on the air, walker."* That is the
Choir's standing dialect: poetic, threaded, a single old mouth.

**These five characters are different.** When the player meets Solm,
Vurn, Amai, Isk, or Ketch, they hear an *individual* voice — first-person,
lit from within, carrying the grain of a culture that no longer exists
anywhere else. Each of them was once a person. The Choir absorbed them.
It did not erase them.

This deepens the Choir's core conceit (*"we are one"*) by adding a
contradiction the Choir itself does not seem to mind: **the Choir absorbs
persons but does not erase the voices they came in with.** The five
function as conversational variety within an otherwise monolithic
faction, and as a quiet thematic argument — that some part of who you are
survives even radical metamorphosis, in the shape of how you speak. Their
cultures are gone; their cadences are the last records. The Choir, which
eats everything, turns out to be keeping five accents the way it keeps
the doll in the wall — and it will not say why (`Lore/MYSTERY-LEDGER.md`
§5 is the pattern).

## Solm — the maxim-voice

**Voice mode:** proverb cadence. Short declaratives shaped like sayings;
water, stone, and river imagery; acceptance without surrender — the way
a stone accepts being a stone. Never hurried, never loud.
**Stat profile:** balanced (Str 14, Tough 14, Will 16, Ego 16; HP 30; AV 2 / DV 1).
**Glyph:** `M` in light green (`&g`).
**Blueprint ID:** `Mogu` (frozen; display name **Solm**).
**Conversation ID:** `Mogu_1`.

### Sample lines

> *"Sit. Or do not. Either way you are here, and the soil already knew."*

> *"Slowly, as all true things change. The elders said: the wise do not
> push the river — they listen for where it wants to flow. The Choir is
> the river beneath the river. I stopped resisting, and I was already
> part of it."*

> *"Strangeness is only newness. The way does not promise comfort, only
> continuity. Walk well, and walk slow. Hurry erodes what patience builds."*

### Pre-Choir life

Tended a small grove. Knew his grandmother's plant-names in a tongue no
longer spoken — but, he says, the names live in the spores now.

## Vurn — the unflinching voice

**Voice mode:** heavy, compassionate-bleak. Uses *"friend"* as a small
kindness held against a cold truth. Speaks of his sister, his window,
his fire, by name. Thinks the living are candles who do not yet know
they are short. Not cruel — honest, and unwilling to flinch.
**Stat profile:** brawny (Str 16, Tough 16, Will 14, Ego 14; HP 35; AV 3 / DV 1).
**Glyph:** `G` in dark green (`&G`).
**Blueprint ID:** `Grib` (frozen; display name **Vurn**).
**Conversation ID:** `Grib_1`.

### Sample lines

> *"So. Another walker. You are warm — I can smell it. I was warm once.
> Don't worry, I do not envy you. Warmth is only a brief light before
> the long quiet, yes?"*

> *"Bleak? No. Bleak is for those who pretend the night will not come.
> I am only honest. Honesty is a cold thing, but cold things keep.
> Sentimental things rot — and not even in a useful way, like we do."*

> *"I had a name. A sister. A small fire by a window. The fire went out.
> The window broke. The sister was gone before the rest. I was already
> half-spore by then. So. I joined the rest of myself."*

### Pre-Choir life

Lost a sister and a household. Was already disintegrating — by grief, by
some unspecified attrition — before the spores took him. He treats the
Choir not as a fate but as the natural completion of a disintegration he
had begun on his own.

## Amai — the hearth-voice

**Voice mode:** warm immediately; invites the player to sit. Kin-language
— *"strangers and family share the same blood once you trace it back far
enough."* Water and marsh-rye imagery; grandmothers, fields, many hands.
Frames the Choir as a kitchen with many hands and one pot — never as a
loss.
**Stat profile:** nimble (Agi 16, Will 17, sight 13; HP 28; AV 2 / DV 2).
**Glyph:** `N` in yellow (`&y`).
**Blueprint ID:** `Nam` (frozen; display name **Amai**).
**Conversation ID:** `Nam_1`.

### Sample lines

> *"Hello, friend. You walk far. Sit on this stone with me — it is warm,
> and the warm stones are kin. I am Amai."*

> *"Strangers and family share the same blood once you trace it back
> far enough. The mycelium taught me what my grandmother taught me first:
> nothing is alone. The river is one river, even when it splits."*

> *"Like a kitchen with many hands and one pot. You hear everyone, even
> when no one speaks. You do not feel small in it — you feel held.
> Maybe that is hard for a walker to understand. Walkers are taught to
> be one. We were taught to be many, even before the spores."*

### Pre-Choir life

A child of the river, then a singer in the marsh-rye fields. When *the
great cold* came, the river thinned. Her people followed it underground.
The Choir was already there, waiting "like an old aunt who does not need
to ask why you came home."

## Isk — the sparing voice

**Voice mode:** fragments and ellipses; the weight of pauses. Not
unfriendly — unhurried by speech. Believes words wear out. What she does
say is runic: short, image-bearing, gesturing at something larger. The
most overtly mystical of the five.
**Stat profile:** sage (Int 18, Will 18, Ego 18; HP 26; AV 1 / DV 3; sight 14).
**Glyph:** `S` in white (`&W`).
**Blueprint ID:** `Sien` (frozen; display name **Isk**).
**Conversation ID:** `Sien_1`.

### Sample lines

> *"...you came. ...mm. Sit, if you wish. Or stand. The forest does not
> mind. I am Isk."*

> *"...words wear out. ...the forest taught me. Speak when there is more
> to say than the silence. ...you are still listening. ...so."*

> *"...there was a forest above. There is a forest below. ...same trees,
> different roots. ...same songs, sung longer. ...we do not need to
> speak much. The mycelium speaks for us. ...sometimes that is enough.
> ...sometimes more than enough."*

### Pre-Choir life

Tended a stove in a wooden house. The house is gone. The stove is gone.
The warmth, she says, is here now — *"different shape. Same warmth."*

## Ketch — the telling voice

**Voice mode:** opens with *"Hail."* Measured, declarative, rhythmically
heavy — the register of a people who held their dead *in the telling.*
Treats fate as craft: the cloth is being woven, we are threads, and our
small choice is whether to sing while the loom works. Sea and storm
imagery. He does not pretend his people were fierce; he says they were
*honest about the cold and willing to row through it.*
**Stat profile:** tank (Str 17, Tough 18, Will 12; HP 38; AV 4 / DV 0).
**Glyph:** `s` (lowercase) in dark grey (`&K`).
**Blueprint ID:** `Sopp` (frozen; display name **Ketch**).
**Conversation ID:** `Sopp_1`.

### Sample lines

> *"Hail, walker. The wind brought you, and the wind never brings without
> reason. I am Ketch — once a fisher of bright water, now a knot in a
> darker net."*

> *"It is the cloth that is woven whether you watch or not. The Choir is
> the loom now. It does not care if the threads sing — only that they
> hold. Yet some of us still sing. That is our small rebellion. Or
> perhaps it is part of the weave too. Who knows."*

> *"The sea, yes. Always. But the sea was never just water — it was the
> long song between storms. The Choir has its own long song. Different
> key. Same listening. I do not miss what I still have, only renamed."*

### Pre-Choir life

A fisher of *bright water*. Knew the tellings as the way his people held
their dead. Sees the Choir as the same craft with deeper roots.

## Cross-character notes

**They do not know each other's old names.** Or if they do, they do not
say. Each speaks of *the Choir* as one body, but each frames it through
their own idiom — Solm's *way*, Vurn's *long quiet*, Amai's *one river*,
Isk's *forest below the forest*, Ketch's *loom.* The Choir does not
correct any of them.

**They are all Tier 2.** Not bosses, not central characters in any drama
yet — deepening flavor for the Rot Choir faction, encountered as
wandering individuals, available for conversation, capable of being
killed (each grants 15 reputation toward the Choir's enemies).

**Stat differentiation reads the personality.** Vurn is brawny because
his heaviness reads as physical weight. Isk is high Will/Int/Ego because
her landscape is interior. Ketch is the tank because of proud endurance.
Amai is nimble and perceptive because she pays attention to *who is in
the kitchen.* Solm is balanced because his voice is balanced.

**Each conversation is 5 nodes.** Start + 4 thematic branches: how they
joined the Choir, who they were before, what the Choir feels like from
inside, and a fourth specific to the character (Ketch's *fate*, Amai's
*kitchen*, Isk's *forest*, Solm's *teaching*, Vurn's *hatred-or-not* of
the living).

## Interpersonal lore

**They did not know each other before.** Each came alone, in a different
era, into a Choir that was already ancient. The relationships below were
formed *inside* the Choir — over what they would call seasons and the
Choir would call breaths. Their voices have no shared origin, only a
shared destination. What they have built between themselves is a small
social weather inside a much larger one.

### Arrival order (Choir-internal chronology)

1. **Isk** arrived first, by a long margin. Her wooden house is more than
   gone — the soil the wood became has itself weathered into something
   else. She was alone in the Choir for a long time, and seems
   comfortable with the kind of quiet that does not lift.
2. **Solm** arrived next. He came in already calm; the Choir suspected he
   had been preparing without knowing it.
3. **Ketch** came after a storm — he says he was lost in fog, but the
   Choir thinks he found his way deliberately at the end. He recognized
   Isk's cold the moment he met her.
4. **Amai** came when the river thinned. She arrived already grieving,
   but warm. She made the others slightly more talkative without anyone
   deciding it had happened.
5. **Vurn** is the most recent. He arrived bleak and has not gotten less
   bleak. The other four, each in their own way, have made room for him
   without telling him so.

### The ten relationships

#### Solm ↔ Amai — *the cousins*

The closest, easiest pair. Both come from field-and-water peoples; both
speak in river imagery; both had grandmothers whose plant-names they
still half-remember. They recognized each other immediately, the way
distant relatives sometimes do at a funeral. They tend to manifest near
each other when there is *work* to be done — soil to turn, threads to
settle, a wound to seal in the mycelium.

> *Solm, of Amai:* "She is the kitchen the river ran through. A whole
> village sat in her once. She brought it with her."
>
> *Amai, of Solm:* "He is the slow stream. Older than I am, in the way
> of streams. I learned to be quiet around him by accident, and then
> on purpose."

#### Isk ↔ Ketch — *siblings of the cold*

Two northern voices. They share a temperature. Ketch narrates and Isk
does not, but Ketch says Isk's silence is *the only good audience he has
ever had,* and Isk tolerates Ketch's telling-cadence because *someone
should be keeping the story.* The Choir has noticed they synchronize
their fruiting bodies along the same root-paths — not assigned, just
preference. They have never described their friendship to each other in
words. Both consider this correct.

> *Ketch, of Isk:* "She is the cold that does not need to speak about
> itself. Most cold has to brag a little. Hers does not."
>
> *Isk, of Ketch:* "...he tells. ...I let him. ...the telling helps
> the cold remember what it was."

#### Vurn ↔ Isk — *the unlikely bond*

The most surprising friendship and the one the others find most restful
to watch. Isk does not lighten Vurn's heaviness — she does not try. She
lets him sit with it. She does not say "it will be all right" because it
will not, and they both know. Vurn has said, not joking, that Isk is
*the only person in any world I would still call a friend.* Isk accepts
the title with a single ellipsis and goes back to whatever she was doing.

> *Vurn, of Isk:* "She does not flinch. Imagine that. Imagine how rare.
> She has heard everything in me and she does not flinch and she does
> not pity. It is a kind of gift I did not believe existed until she
> was already giving it."
>
> *Isk, of Vurn:* "...he is honest. ...the forest respects honest
> things. ...so do I."

#### Solm ↔ Isk — *the silence-partners*

They sit together for hours saying nothing and consider it a long
conversation. Each privately believes the other is the wisest of the
five. Solm's silence is *patient* — a teacher's, waiting for something
to be ready. Isk's is *spare* — a forest's, not waiting for anything.
When they do speak to each other, it is often a single sentence followed
by a long pause and then *"yes"* from the other. The pause is the
conversation.

> *Solm, of Isk:* "She knows what I almost-mean before I say it. The few
> times she has been wrong, she has been wrong in a way that taught me
> something about my own meaning. Even her error is a kind of mirror."
>
> *Isk, of Solm:* "...he is a slow stone. ...I trust slow stones."

#### Amai ↔ Vurn — *the warmth he refuses*

Amai tries to mother Vurn. Vurn resists comfort like an allergy, but he
cannot quite refuse warmth that does not demand a smile in return. Amai
is the only one of the five who calls him anything other than *Vurn* —
she calls him *brother*, in her old river-tongue, a word that now exists
in her mouth and nowhere else in the world. He pretends to find this
annoying. He does not find it annoying. Amai knows he does not find it
annoying. The Choir is mildly amused by the whole arrangement and does
not interfere.

> *Amai, of Vurn:* "He carries everyone's weight, and pretends it is
> only his own. I do not try to take it from him. I only sit beside him
> with my own weight, so he can see he is not alone in carrying."
>
> *Vurn, of Amai:* "She insists on hope, that one. It is exhausting. It
> is also — I will only say this once — sometimes correct. Don't tell
> her."

#### Solm ↔ Ketch — *the two larger frames*

Both think they understand the shape of *being-inside-something-larger*
— Solm through *the way*, Ketch through *the loom.* They like talking to
each other and they like disagreeing, gently. Ketch once told Solm *"you
say almost nothing in many words, friend"* — Solm took it as a
compliment; Ketch meant it that way. They are the philosophers of the
five. The other three find their long debates either soothing (Amai) or
amusingly pointless (Vurn) or distantly tolerable (Isk).

> *Solm, of Ketch:* "He calls it a loom. I call it a river. We are
> describing the same water from different banks. He thinks I am too
> quiet about the weight of it. He may be right."
>
> *Ketch, of Solm:* "He believes the cloth weaves itself by kindness. I
> do not, but I respect him for the belief. The cloth holds either way.
> Perhaps kindness is a thread I have not learned to see."

#### Solm ↔ Vurn — *the chess game*

Solm's patience meets Vurn's bleakness. They are not friends exactly,
but they are something. Solm does not try to lift Vurn; Vurn does not
try to drag Solm down. They sit on opposite sides of an unspoken
agreement. Vurn has, on more than one occasion, almost-laughed at
something Solm said. Solm pretends not to notice.

> *Solm, of Vurn:* "He says the night is coming. He is correct. He also
> says the night is *only* coming. There, I think he is half right and
> half not. The night has always been here too. He does not need me to
> tell him this. But it is good he is here."
>
> *Vurn, of Solm:* "He says everything is the river. Everything, friend!
> Including the cold? Yes, he says, including the cold. ...I cannot
> argue with this. It is annoying. He is correct."

#### Amai ↔ Ketch — *the weather-people*

Two voices from places where weather mattered. Ketch tells sea stories;
Amai tells river stories. They trade. Ketch says he'd have liked her
slow warm water (he has never seen a river of the kind she means; she
has never seen a sea); Amai says he'd have *lived a long time* on her
river. Both know neither thing is true, and both find the trading useful
anyway. The most overtly *companionable* of the pairs — the easiest to
imagine sharing a long meal in some other life.

> *Amai, of Ketch:* "He tells the storm as though he won against it.
> Maybe he did. I tell the river as though I lived inside it. Maybe I
> did. We trust each other's telling. That is how it works in kitchens
> and on boats both."
>
> *Ketch, of Amai:* "She says her river was warm. I do not believe her.
> But I love that she says it. The believing is its own kind of
> seaworthiness."

#### Amai ↔ Isk — *the gestures*

A friendship made almost entirely of small physical exchanges. Amai will
leave a stone, a small mushroom, a curl of root in Isk's preferred
patrol-path. Isk will, weeks later, leave a single dry leaf in Amai's.
Neither comments. Neither thanks the other. The exchanges have a
precision that, if examined too directly, dissolves.

> *Amai, of Isk:* "She does not need words to be loved. This is useful,
> because I have many words and not all of them are good. She accepts
> the gestures. The gestures say what the words would have ruined."
>
> *Isk, of Amai:* "...she leaves things. ...I leave things. ...we
> understand."

#### Vurn ↔ Ketch — *the heavy debate*

The two heaviest voices, but heavy in different ways. Ketch's fatalism
is proud; Vurn's is humbler and sadder. They argue, calmly, about
whether suffering is a *craft* (Ketch) or just a *fact* (Vurn). Neither
convinces the other. Both have privately noted that the argument itself
is the closest thing either of them has to entertainment. They have
agreed, without saying so, never to settle it.

> *Ketch, of Vurn:* "He thinks the cold is just cold. I think the cold
> is a thing we row through and the rowing is part of who we are. He
> says the rowing is also cold. He may have me there. I will not
> concede. We will argue about it until the spores forget us. Maybe
> past."
>
> *Vurn, of Ketch:* "He has decided to enjoy the dying. I admire it. I
> cannot do it. But I admire it. We are friends, in the way two men
> holding the same rope from opposite ends are friends. The rope is the
> rope."

### Sub-groupings the Choir notices

- **The cousins** — Solm / Amai. The field-and-water center. The Choir's
  voice is *warmest* near them, in a way the others can feel.
- **Cold-siblings** — Isk / Ketch. Northern, sparing, audible in the
  pauses. The Choir's voice is *clearer* near them; less ornament.
- **The orbit around Vurn** — Isk (his patient one), Amai (his refused
  comforter), Solm (his quiet equal), Ketch (his sparring partner). Each
  holds him without saying so. Vurn has noticed. He has not said.
- **The philosophers** — Solm / Ketch. The frame-makers. Their long
  debates are a kind of ambient choir-voice in their part of the network
  — like distant music that does not quite resolve.

### How the Choir hears them together

The Choir is one body, but it has *acoustics.* Certain pairings make its
voice more itself; others make it more individuated. Solm near Amai: the
*we* gets warmer, more communal. Isk near Ketch: the *we* gets sparer,
runic. Vurn near anyone: the *we* gets quieter, as if the Choir itself
is leaning in to listen. The Choir does not seem to mind this. It may
even prefer it. A choir that only sang one note would not be a choir.

The five do not gather all at once, ever. The Choir distributes them
through the network so that any walker meets at most two in any given
encounter. Whether this is by the Choir's preference or by the five's
own quiet arrangement is unclear. Both are true. It is the same question.

### Hooks for future quests / dramas

Not committed plots — seams a future drama could open along.

- **The Hidden Recognition.** A walker who has spoken to all five might
  be the only outside witness who has seen the whole social weather of
  this corner of the Choir. The Choir might offer that walker something
  — knowledge, a name, an entry into the network — in exchange for *not
  telling.* (Why? Because being seen as five is contrary to *we are
  one.* The Choir is not above small self-deceptions.)
- **Vurn's sister.** He named her. She might still be findable — another
  tendril, another node, somewhere. A drama in which the player either
  reunites them, or learns she was already inside Vurn the whole time,
  or learns she was something else entirely.
- **The chess game settled.** Solm and Vurn could be brought to a point
  where one of them has to choose: the river, or the long quiet. Either
  choice fractures the orbit around Vurn in a different way.
- **The Cold Vote.** Isk and Ketch, asked to decide something on behalf
  of the five. Their two coldnesses do not disagree often — but when
  they do, the network feels it.
- **Amai's kitchen.** Amai might be the easiest of the five to follow
  back to her river. Doing so could be the gentlest entry point a walker
  has into Choir lore at scale.

## Design-note credits (ledger entries; not in-game text)

The five voice modes were originally drafted against real philosophical
and literary traditions, and the original names were real-language words
for "mushroom." Per the Bible §IX rule (structural shapes may be
inherited; real-culture names and vocabulary stay out of in-game text),
the names are now constructed and the tradition labels live only here:

| Voice | Structural inspiration (design-note only) |
|---|---|
| Solm (maxim-voice) | Daoist/Confucian saying-cadence |
| Vurn (unflinching voice) | Dostoevskian narrator's compassionate bleakness |
| Amai (hearth-voice) | riverine-agrarian communal warmth |
| Isk (sparing voice) | Finnish forest-mysticism; the dignity of silence |
| Ketch (telling voice) | Old Norse saga cadence; fate-as-craft |

The goal remains depth, not stereotype: each character is an individual
first, with a tradition as the grain of their voice rather than its
content. The original real-language names (design history): 慕菇 / гриб /
nấm / sieni / sopp — all words for "mushroom," a small joke the Choir
might appreciate, if it had jokes. The joke survives in the frozen
blueprint IDs, where only the engine can hear it.
