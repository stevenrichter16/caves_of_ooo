## C1 — Urqu is pressure, never agent

### The commit

**Urqu is weather, not will.** The empty seventh position exerts
incarnation-pressure along a gradient — toward whatever un-names,
wherever abandonment pools — and that is *all* it does. It has no
continuous self, no desire, no plan, no patience, no hunger. Every
sentence in prior canon of the form "Urqu wants / uses / offers /
tries" is superseded by gradient-language: pressure flows, pools,
finds channels, deforms what it passes through.

**Mouthpieces carry the intent.** A mouthpiece is a person (or a
lesser numen) deformed by standing too close to a near-incarnation.
The proto-divine power that leaks through them is real; the bargains
they offer are real; but the *wanting* in the bargain is the
mouthpiece's own — their greed, their grief, their theory of what the
pressure is. Two mouthpieces can offer contradictory bargains in the
same season, because there is no central will to contradict.

### Why this and not the alternative

The alternative — commit to Urqu-as-agent and delete the "no agency to
direct" line — was considered and rejected. An antagonist that
schemes is a devil, and devils are a solved problem in fantasy; an
antagonist that is *only a gradient* keeps the cosmic-horror register
honest (there is nothing to argue with, nothing to blame) and makes
the mouthpieces scarier, not less: **there is no devil making them do
it. It is only people, leaking.**

### Ripples

- The Bloom link simplifies: the Bloom multiplies abandonment (C4);
  abandonment steepens the gradient. No intent anywhere in the loop.
- Bargain design (Phase 5 §IV) survives intact, re-attributed:
  corruption marks, ink-drinking, Recanting-shaped reversal — all now
  the mouthpiece's ritual grammar, which varies by mouthpiece.
- **Language rule (lintable):** canon and in-world text may not attach
  intent-verbs to Urqu. Grep gate at M8. In-world speakers *may*
  violate this — superstition is characterization — but the narration
  and the design layer may not.

## C2 — The Strike installs a chooser, not a function

### The commit

Naming cannot be installed. So why do vessel-path Renewals work at
all, even for a while? **Because the Strike does not install Naming —
it installs a *chooser*.** A struck vessel holds Naming exactly as
long as it keeps *choosing* to enact it: enactment smuggled inside
installation. The vessel wakes every day — for centuries — and says
yes again.

The crack is that **choice cannot be inherited.** The vessel ages into
its function as the Six did; the yes wears grooves; one far
generation, the choosing stops being a choice and becomes a
habit, and a habit cannot hold Naming, and the Thinning returns. No
successor can re-choose *for* the vessel, because a choice made on
someone's behalf is an installation, which is where we came in.

The practice-path is the same fact at population scale: **a world of
mortals never stops choosing, because it never stops being replaced.**
Every generation's yes is fresh because every generation is new.
Mortality — the thing the Tree's plan treated as the problem — is the
load-bearing feature. The gods were the mistake.

### Why this and not the alternative

The alternative — weaken the "installation is incoherent" argument so
vessel-paths can just work — was rejected because Naro's refusal is
the setting's moral center and the argument is his whole reason. This
commit keeps the philosophy at full strength *and* explains the
vessel-path's temporary function with one mechanism, stated in the
world's own vocabulary.

### Ripples

- The Six are reread, not retconned: each of them is a chooser whose
  yes wore grooves. The Wedded stopped being able to distinguish her
  yes from the substrate's; the Salted filed hers; Tovreth signs his
  daily. Their transformations are what a millennium of compelled
  re-choosing looks like — which is why none of them can hold the
  seventh position either.
- "Urqu is answered" is de-mystified (see C3): universal practice
  drains the pressure at its source; nothing "becomes" anything.
- Saedis's teaching (Phase 7) gains its exact content: not a secret,
  a *discipline* — how to keep a yes a choice.

## C3 — The practice-path Renewal gets permanent costs

### The commit

The hierarchy language is struck: no document may call any ending
best, true, or canonical-preferred. The vessel-path / practice-path
distinction is mechanical (one cracks, one doesn't), not moral. And
the practice-path pays three permanent, witnessable costs:

1. **The gods end.** Universalizing Naming redistributes the six
   inherited functions back into the world's common practice. The Six
   begin — for the first time in ~1,080 years — to age. Maeleth and
   Othren's reconciliation, if the player earned it, is also a
   farewell; Tovreth's long-promised rest arrives as the pantheon's
   fate, not his exception; Ylaes's resin gives; Selen's distributed
   voice thins to one grove; Dohren goes last, because roots do. The
   renewed world loses its gods **and the last living witnesses of the
   Tree.** To heal the world, you must let the people who remember it
   end.
2. **The work never finishes.** The *sari... sari...* does not stop.
   It changes pitch — from the sound of naming failing to the sound of
   naming being done, continuously, everywhere, forever. If any
   generation stops, the Thinning returns. The practice-path is not a
   fix; it is a chore bequeathed to everyone, always. Hope as burden.
3. **The Root ends as itself.** Waking without fear means dissolving
   into the new binding. The Tree's last fragment stops being a self.
   The wall Dohren has reached toward for a thousand years is, at
   last, warm — and then it is only a wall. His arc's real question
   was never whether his arms would close the distance; it is whether
   he can want this *for the Root* anyway.

### What the other endings gain

- **Preserve** is now *the only ending in which the Six survive as
  themselves.* A player who has come to love the gods — and the game
  works hard to make the gods loveable — has a real reason to stop the
  world.
- **Consume** is now *the only ending in which nothing is ever lost.*
  Every self unbound, every thread kept. The practice-path world
  forgets things daily, the way living worlds do; the Choir's world
  never forgets anything again.
- The three endings restate as **three verdicts on grief**: keep it
  (Preserve), dissolve it (Consume), or finish it (Renewal). Finishing
  grief means the griefs *end* — and things that end are gone. None of
  the three is endorsed. The epilogues carry the costs without
  adjudicating them.

### Why this and not the alternative

The alternative — keep one clean-win ending and accept the hierarchy —
was rejected because the corpus's own thesis ("all morally complex,
none cost-free") was right the first time; the execution just broke
faith with it. The fix honors the original bet.

## C4 — The closure-ledger replaces the completion-ratio

### The commit

What feeds the pressure is not *incompletion* — it is **abandonment:
an act left dangling without a spoken end.** The world-physics
variable is renamed the **closure-ledger**, and it counts three states
for every undertaken act:

| State | Definition | Cosmic effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed** | Carried through to its finish | drains the gradient |
| **Refused** | Ended by a deliberate, *enacted* no — declined to the giver's face, formally defaulted, renounced by ritual | **drains the gradient** (a spoken no is a closure) |
| **Abandoned** | Dropped in silence; ghosted; left dangling | feeds the gradient |

A player may therefore walk a principled refuser's path through the
whole game — declining quests, renouncing pacts, saying every no out
loud — and keep a clean ledger. The Apatheia trap resolves into a real
choice instead of a hidden penalty: refusal *enacted* is stillness;
refusal *by avoidance* is entropy. The Spirit of stillness itself
recognizes the difference — its gifts favor the player who declines
aloud over the one who slips away.

**Ending gate change:** the practice-path Renewal requires a clean
closure-ledger, not a high completion count. A completionist and a
principled refuser can both earn it; a ghost cannot. *You cannot teach
the world to finish its names while leaving your own endings
unspoken.*

### The Naro retcon (small, load-bearing)

**Naro refused aloud, in the circle, before the first Strike.** The
Tree — senile, committed, already falling into its plan — had no
category for a no and did not register it. The six heard it. **Their
six accounts of what Naro said do not agree** (this feeds the Phase-11
epistemic re-opening; see `Lore/Design/NaroReadings.md`). The account
most often treated as definitive is Maeleth's — and hers is the least
reliable of the six, *because* she is the god of Memory: she has
re-copied that moment ten thousand times, and every copying is an
edit. The archivist's memory is the most-edited memory in the world.

This retcon supersedes the Spine's "the Tree learned about the seventh
only at the moment of falling" — the Tree *never* learned; learning
requires a category for refusal, and the Tree's senility is precisely
that it had stopped having one. The tragedy sharpens: the world's
first spoken no was also the world's first unheard one.

### Why this and not the alternative

The alternative — keep the ratio and add exceptions for "good"
refusals — was rejected as a morality meter with a whitelist. The
closed/refused/abandoned triad is not a moral scale; it is a
*grammatical* one (did the act end in a sentence or trail off?), which
is the correct physics for a world whose substance is Naming.

### Ripples

- Diag instrumentation: `category=closure` records with
  `kind=Closed|Refused|Abandoned` and a `spoken` payload field.
  Engineering work, out of lore scope, noted for the handoff.
- Rentals, debts, Held Breaths, conversations: each needs a designed
  "spoken no" affordance (a decline verb, not just a walk-away).
  Level-design handoff.
- The Concord's texture improves for free: a formal default is a
  closure — costly in reputation, clean in cosmology. The Concord has
  paperwork for saying no. Of course it does.
