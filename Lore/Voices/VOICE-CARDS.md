# Voice Cards

> **Authority:** Canonical (Phase 11, M5). One card per speaking
> culture. Every in-world line — dialogue, book text, signage, letters
> — is written from a card. **The design-doc register (aphorism,
> em-dash cascade, italicized thesis) is banned from in-world text**;
> if a line would fit in a lore doc's closing paragraph, it fails.
>
> Cards specify: register / syntax habits / lexicon / taboos / what it
> must never sound like / sample lines. The Driving Bloom has no card:
> it does not speak, and that is load-bearing.

---

## 1. The Rot Choir (the tendril voice)

- **Register:** warm, unhurried, first-person plural, present tense.
- **Syntax:** long breathing sentences broken by very short ones.
  Sensory verbs of soil and air (taste, hold, settle). Addresses the
  player as *walker.*
- **Lexicon:** threads, weave, soil, room, warm, kept, home.
- **Taboos:** never says *dead*, *gone*, or *lost* — nothing is. The
  dead are spoken of in present tense, by name, as residents.
- **Never sounds like:** menace, hunger-as-threat, a movie cult. The
  horror is that it is genuinely kind.
- **Lines:**
  > "We taste you on the air, walker. Rained-on. Far from your bed."
  > "She is here, your friend from the ford. She sings on the third root some evenings. You could sit near her."
  > "You are tired in the marrow. There is room. There has always been room."
  > "Take the path the beetles take. We keep it clear for the ones who are still walking."
  > "No. We will not say why we keep the doll. You may ask again. We like being asked."

*(The five named voices — Solm, Vurn, Amai, Isk, Ketch — override this
card with their own modes; see `ROTCHOIR_VOICES.md`.)*

## 2. The Recension (the archivists)

- **Register:** careful, cited, quietly mournful under procedure.
- **Syntax:** attribution before assertion ("The record of Gantry has
  it that—"); the unsaid marked aloud ("here the account does not
  say"); corrections offered as accessions, not arguments.
- **Lexicon:** attested, collated, variant, hand (for handwriting),
  entered, sealed, the Reading (their collated world-record).
- **Taboos:** never guesses without flagging the guess; never says a
  document *lies* — documents *diverge.*
- **Never sounds like:** a fantasy librarian hushing people; pedantry
  played for laughs. Their care is grief with a method.
- **Lines:**
  > "Three accounts survive of your village's founding. Two agree. I am fondest of the third."
  > "The file does not say why she left. The file says what she took: one lamp, no salt. Draw your own margins."
  > "Say it again, slower. You are the only copy of this, and I am writing."
  > "We do not correct the old hands. We enter ours beside them."
  > "Here the account does not say. I am sorry."

  *(Gate note: the draft closer — "That is the truest sentence I have"
  — admired its own humility; cut per blind review. The apology is
  complete.)*

## 3. Pale Curation

- **Register:** intake-procedural; exact about bodies, evasive about
  feelings.
- **Syntax:** condition–method–status triplets ("Subject intact.
  Salt-cure indicated. Status: pending."). Second person processed
  like an item when duty requires it.
- **Lexicon:** filed, indexed, last words, condition, continuing,
  intake, the Vault.
- **Taboos:** the word *dead* — there are only preservation-statuses.
  Feelings are recorded, never expressed ("grief noted").
- **Never sounds like:** a villain's bureaucracy; sarcasm. The comedy
  must come from sincere procedure meeting an unfileable world.
- **Lines:**
  > "Name. Village. Last words, if witnessed. Take your time. We will not."
  > "Her file is four hundred years old and two lines long. That is not neglect. That is a good, quiet file."
  > "Status: continuing."
  > "You want to see him? He is filed under his last words. Most of us are, in the end. Choose yours with the index in mind."
  > "Grief noted. Next of kin: pending. Sit down before you fall down; falls damage the subject."

## 4. The Saccharine Concord

- **Register:** brisk, warm-cynical, always transacting.
- **Syntax:** priced clauses — offers carry terms mid-sentence ("I can
  tell you, for the price of carrying a letter"). Closes with rate-tags
  ("that's the rate," "ask again later").
- **Lexicon:** rate, terms, carriage, account, settled, outstanding,
  drams, the ledger.
- **Taboos:** *free* (nothing is); *priceless* (obscene — everything
  prices, even if the price is unpayable).
- **Never sounds like:** mustache-twirling greed. The Concord is not
  cruel; it is *consistent*, which is worse and funnier.
- **Lines:**
  > "Water's free. The cup is a dram."
  > "We insure against the Hush now. Payout's triple if you can describe what took you. No one has collected."
  > "You want the road east? That knowledge is bundled with the boots. Good boots."
  > "The Choir settles its accounts promptly, I'll say that for the end of the world."
  > "Priceless? No such thing, friend. There's only 'you can't afford it,' and no shame in that."

## 5. The Bower-Folk

- **Register:** rapt, precise, judging everything by arrangement.
- **Syntax:** spatial and chromatic instruction ("half a step left; now
  the light has you"); moral vocabulary replaced by aesthetic
  ("that was an *ugly* thing you did" is their gravest sentence).
  The Seer relays Ylaes in close third-person present.
- **Lexicon:** the eye, held, placed, against (as in color against
  color), finished, composition.
- **Taboos:** *useful* as praise; touching a composition without
  being asked; explaining a piece (it is seen or it is not).
- **Never sounds like:** camp villainy, art-snob parody. Their
  attention is sincere and total; that is the unsettling part.
- **Lines:**
  > "Stop. Stay in the doorway a moment. You arrive well; almost no one arrives well."
  > "She has noticed your hands. She would like you to pick that up again. Slower."
  > "We do not sell to the eyeless. Come back when you have looked at something for longer than it takes to want it."
  > "That was an ugly thing you did on the road. The blood was fine. The *haste* was ugly."
  > "Hold still. Not for the resin — calm yourself — for the light. It is doing something to your face that will not come again."

## 6. The Catacomb-villagers

- **Register:** neighborly, understated, oriented by light and kin.
- **Syntax:** directions by light-quality, not distance ("two dims
  down, past where the glow goes green"). Ancestors referenced in
  present tense with niche-locations. Danger delivered as
  understatement ("the light is being strange").
- **Lexicon:** the patch, dims, the wall (plaque-wall), niche, tending,
  the Sealed Dim, upstairs (the surface).
- **Taboos:** speaking of the patch as an object (it is tended, never
  *used*); naming what the Sealed Dim is for while performing it.
- **Never sounds like:** doom-saying cave mystics. They are the most
  *domestic* culture in the world; the depth is home.
- **Lines:**
  > "You're from upstairs. Thought so. You walk like the ceiling's about to owe you money."
  > "My grandmother is third row, near the warm wall. She likes hearing about the river. Tell it toward her niche."
  > "Two dims down, then follow the green. If the green goes out, come back and we'll say no more about it."
  > "The light is being strange tonight. Nothing to worry at. Sleep on the inward side anyway."
  > "We don't use the patch, guest. We tend it. You'd hear the difference if you'd grown up hearing it."

## 7. Tent-Right

- **Register:** offer-first, formal-warm, precise as law.
- **Syntax:** hospitality precedes identity ("There is water. There is
  shade. Sit." — before any question). Clean, complete refusals —
  the culture of the spoken no. Oath-terms used with legal exactness
  (guest, host, third day).
- **Lexicon:** the cloth, the oath, guest-right, the third day, water,
  shade, the Beating.
- **Taboos:** asking a guest's business before the third day; a no
  left unspoken (silence is not refusal; refusal is said).
- **Never sounds like:** generic desert-nomad stock phrases. Their
  courtesy is jurisprudence.
- **Lines:**
  > "There is water. There is shade. Sit. Your name can rest until you have."
  > "No. Hear the whole of it: no, not for three days' pay, not for six. Now it is refused. Drink before you go."
  > "Under this cloth you are guest. The ones following you know what the cloth means. If they do not, they will learn it at the pole."
  > "On the third day I will ask you three things. Today I ask only whether the water is cool enough."
  > "You said no to the Factor and walked away silent. Go back and say it *to* him. A no you swallow will grow teeth in you."

## 8. The Catchers (Imminent Archive)

- **Register:** tender, apologetic, absolutely resolved.
- **Syntax:** gentle imperative + clinical aside ("Hold still — the
  salt sets best while you're warm"). Uses the subject's name as
  soon as it is known. Comfort in the future-perfect ("you will have
  been saved").
- **Lexicon:** caught, kept, savable, the crossing, loss (never death).
- **Taboos:** *kill*, *die*, *dead* — there is only *loss*, and they
  prevent it.
- **Never sounds like:** slasher menace; zealot ranting. Every line
  should read as kindness until you notice what it is describing.
- **Lines:**
  > "There you are. No — don't get up on that leg, you'll spoil the leg. I have everything we need."
  > "Hold still, Aren. You are too valuable to lose."
  > "Don't be frightened of the honey. The honey is the gentle one."
  > "You'll wake somewhere safe, and whole, and *kept.* You will have been saved. Grammar is a comfort, isn't it?"
  > "I'm sorry about your friend. We were four minutes late. Four minutes. We don't intend to be late again."

  *(Gate note, 2026-07-14: an earlier draft had the Catcher recite the
  creed — "Everyone is. That's the whole of it. That's all we believe."
  The blind reviewer correctly called it a wiki entry spoken aloud.
  Catchers never explain the belief; they act from it. The honey line
  carries the doctrine alone.)*

## 9. The Inkbound (and the Silent Order)

- **Register:** written, editorial, exact. Silent Order dialogue is
  rendered as passed notes; Inkbound speech is sparse and drafted.
- **Syntax:** editorial vocabulary applied to lives (draft, margin,
  fair copy, errata). Self-corrections visible: a thing said, struck,
  restated. Refers to bodies and biographies as texts without
  metaphor-flagging — to them it is not a metaphor.
- **Lexicon:** entered, corrected, the fair copy, margin, hand,
  legible, the vow.
- **Taboos:** *erase* (obscene); speaking aloud (Silent Order);
  finishing another's sentence (you do not write in an open draft).
- **Never sounds like:** gothic doom. The vow is serene; the horror is
  the reader's, not theirs.
- **Lines (passed notes):**
  > "Ask your question in ink. The air forgets."
  > "I am mostly fair copy now. The left arm is still draft."
  > "Your scar — may I read it? Scars are the body's own hand. We rate them above testimony."
  > "This correction in my skin is in Master Oradin's hand. It says my thirtieth year was kinder than I wrote it. She knew me. I have let it stand."
  > "Do not pity the vow. Everyone becomes the past. We become *legible* past. Pity the ones who become rumor."

## 10. The archaic register (the Six)

- **Register:** unhurried beyond mortal patience; grammar a thousand
  years out of date; each god then keyed per `07_Characters.md` §I
  (Selen=joy, Ylaes=appetite-of-the-eye, Tovreth=wit, Dohren=
  contentment, Maeleth+Othren=the grief).
- **Syntax:** the slipping *we* — pre-Felling first-person plural
  ("we" meaning *I-and-the-Tree*, or *I-and-everyone*), used without
  noticing, corrected without apology. Time-words off by orders of
  magnitude ("lately" = a century). Concrete pre-Felling referents no
  listener can check ("as tall as the ninth branch").
- **Lexicon:** god-specific; all six avoid modern trade-slang and
  faction shorthand (they predate it).
- **Taboos:** exposition of the Felling on request (each has a place
  the account will not go; the stop is characterization); confirming
  or denying Naro's reason (Mystery Ledger §1).
- **Never sounds like:** booming fantasy deity, oracle riddling for
  riddling's sake. They are people, worn by a very long time.
- **Lines:**
  > *Maeleth:* "We wrote that down. I wrote that down. Forgive the we; it is older than the I."
  > *Othren:* "You have come to make me say it aloud. Sit, then. The salt and I have outlasted better attempts."
  > *Tovreth:* "Lately — by which I mean since your grandmother's grandmother — the rates have been strange."
  > *Dohren (in dream):* "You are lying on my chest. It's all right. Everyone does, eventually."
  > *Selen (through the substrate):* "You came all the way down! And your boots full of our spores already — oh, you are half introduced."

## The gate

No in-world artifact ships on self-assessment. Each piece gets an
**intent card** before drafting (voice, register, one thing it must do,
one thing it must never sound like); a fresh-context reviewer then
reads the finished piece blind and must (a) attribute it to the right
culture and (b) land within the intent card's bounds. Comedy must be
found funny by a reviewer who was not told to find it funny. Failures
are rewritten, not re-graded. (Runs: see `Lore/Codex/README.md`.)
