# The Three Readings of Naro

> **Authority:** Canonical design document (Phase 11, M2). Implements
> the two-tier truth policy for the Seventh mystery (Mystery Ledger
> §1). Writers and quest designers work from this file; **no content
> derived from it may confirm a reading.**

---

## The two-tier truth policy

- **Design-canon** (this tier): facts the systems need. Here: *struck
  vessels hold Naming only while they keep choosing, and choice cannot
  be inherited* (Bible §IV, C2). This is mechanical truth — it
  determines how vessel-path Renewals behave — and it is **never
  narrated as Naro's vindication.** The crack manifests generations
  beyond the game's horizon; no playthrough observes it.
- **Witnessable canon**: what any in-world text, speaker, or epilogue
  may assert. At this tier the Seventh mystery consists of **evidence,
  testimony, and three living readings.** The words "Naro was right"
  may appear in-game only in a speaker's mouth, as belief.

The finale (see `Lore/Design/V1-DramaticCore.md`) asks the player to
**stake the Renewal's Naming-resolution on a reading.** Different
stakes color the ritual differently. None is graded. The world
answers commitment, not correctness — which is, quietly, the
practice-path's whole argument enacted at the meta level.

---

## Reading A — the Refusal of Understanding
*(held by: the Declined; the Namers; Saedis's family memory)*

**The claim.** Naro alone understood Naming — that it cannot be
installed, only enacted — and refused the Strike to spare the world a
seventh god that would have failed worse than no god at all. The
refusal was the most responsible act in the history of the world, and
it has been blamed for everything for a thousand years.

**The evidence for it.**
- Tent-Right's oath *works*: mortal naming-by-choice demonstrably
  binds (sanctuary holds; the Hush recedes from kept tents). If
  Naming-as-practice is real, Naro's theory has a working model.
- The Declined's unbroken discipline: a family that has declined
  every inheritance for thirty-six generations is itself a kind of
  proof-of-concept — the yes kept fresh by being re-chosen.
- The Sealed Libraries' Naro-file (read one way): a scholar's
  marginalia on the Strike-ritual, in Naro's hand, annotating the
  seventh Strike's design with the single recurring gloss *"it will
  not hold."*

**The problem with it.** Every strand of this evidence passes through
people who *need* it to be true. Saedis's memory is an inheritance —
held by people whose whole tradition is refusing inheritances, and
who have never seen the irony as disqualifying. The oath binding
proves mortal naming works; it does not prove Naro *knew* that.

## Reading B — the First Unsaying
*(held by: a sincere Recension counter-history; some Curation Sorters)*

**The claim.** The moment a chosen mortal stood in the circle and
said *no* to the world's own plan is precisely when the slippage
began. Naro's refusal was not the diagnosis of the wound — it *was*
the wound: the first act in the world's history to un-name a
cosmological position, and Urqu is its echo, still ringing. The
pressure is not the absence the Tree left; it is the absence *Naro
made.*

**The evidence for it.**
- Chronology: no record of *sari... sari...* predates the Felling —
  and the oldest attestations cluster around the Felling-Site, not
  the Root. The slippage radiates from where the no was spoken.
- The same Libraries' Naro-file (read the other way): the marginal
  gloss *"it will not hold"* reads, in period grammar, equally as
  prediction — or as **intention.**
- The six gods' accounts of the refusal disagree (C4) — which a
  Reading-B scholar takes as evidence that the moment itself was
  already slipping, already un-said, as the no left Naro's mouth.

**The problem with it.** It is the reading most comfortable to the
institutions that spent a millennium blaming Naro; comfort is not
evidence. And it must explain why the Tent-Right oath — naming-by-
refusal's own descendants — *strengthens* binding rather than
thinning it. Its scholars' answer ("a controlled burn") has never
satisfied even all of them.

## Reading C — the Empty Summons
*(held by: a Concord actuarial heresy; whispered, never filed)*

**The claim.** There was never a seventh summons at all. The Tree,
senile, dreamed six and believed seven; the "empty place in the
circle" is a story the guilty Six told afterward — because a missing
traitor is easier to survive than a miscounting god. Naro existed
(the scholar of Naming is documented) but was never chosen; the
blame was retrofitted onto the nearest name with the right
speciality. Urqu is not an un-filled position; it is the *seam* of a
lie six people have kept for a thousand years.

**The evidence for it.**
- Tovreth's ledger: the Concord's founding accounts, in the
  Counter's own early hand, record provisioning for a ritual party
  of **six**. When asked, Tovreth neither confirms nor denies —
  which readers of all three readings cite as evidence.
- No pre-Felling document independently attests the seventh summons;
  every source traces to the Six or their institutions.
- The Tree's senility is *everyone's* canon — Reading C merely takes
  it seriously one step earlier than the others do.

**The problem with it.** It requires the six most-watched beings in
history to have kept one story straight for a millennium — and the
one thing the corpus establishes about their accounts is that they
*don't* agree (C4). Its counter-argument — that the disagreements are
the lie eroding — is unfalsifiable, which its own actuaries admit
with professional discomfort.

## Handling rules for writers

1. **Every piece of Seventh-mystery evidence must be attributable** —
   a document with a keeper, a memory with an heir, a ledger with an
   accountant. No omniscient narration.
2. **Maeleth's account is the least reliable and the most cited.**
   The god of Memory has re-copied the refusal ten thousand times;
   every copy is an edit. Use this — it is the corpus's best
   unreliable-narrator device — but never let her (or anyone)
   *settle* it.
3. **Each reading must stay winnable.** If playtesting shows players
   converging on one reading as "obviously" correct, add evidence to
   the others; never subtract.
4. **The Recanting question.** A player who staked a reading may
   come to doubt it. Doubt is content; confirmation is not.
