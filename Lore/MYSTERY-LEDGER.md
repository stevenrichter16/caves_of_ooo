# The Mystery Ledger

> **Authority:** Canonical (Phase 11, M2). This document lists the
> questions the canon **promises never to answer.**
>
> **The protection rule:** no future document, quest, NPC, epilogue,
> item description, or dialogue line may close a ledgered question.
> Evidence, readings, and rumors about them are encouraged — that is
> what they are *for* — but confirmation is forbidden, at both the
> witnessable tier and (except where noted) the design tier. Amending
> this ledger requires a deliberate canon amendment with the removal
> reasoned in writing; "a quest needed it" is not a reason, because
> the quest can want evidence instead.
>
> **Why this exists:** mystery is a non-renewable resource. A corpus
> that answers everything converts mythology into backstory. These
> entries are the canon's permanent reserve — protected precisely
> because they are load-bearing enough that someone will eventually
> be tempted to spend them.

---

## The entries

### 1. Why Naro refused — and whether Naro was right

The big one. Three living readings, each with authored evidence, none
adjudicated in-world — see `Lore/Design/NaroReadings.md`. **Design-tier
exception:** the systems know vessel-paths crack (Bible §IV, C2); the
*reason* — Naro's understanding, Naro's cowardice, or Naro's
never-having-been-summoned — is never confirmed at either tier. The
finale asks the player to stake the ending on a reading; the world
answers the stake without grading it.

**Forbidden from answering:** every document; every god, including
Maeleth (whose account is canonically the most-edited); Saedis (who
carries a *family memory*, which is testimony, not proof); the Sealed
Libraries (whose Naro-file supports two readings depending on how it
is read).

### 2. What the Root dreams when it does not dream the Tree

The bio-light carries the Tree-dreams; the villagers read those. But
there are nights — every Plaque-Tender knows them — when the light
moves in patterns that match nothing in a thousand years of omen
records. No document says what those dreams are.

**Forbidden from answering:** the Rooted (he keeps some of the Root's
dreams *for the Root* — this is already canon and is now protected).

### 3. Whether the Tree was Inquiry's masterpiece

Already soft-open (Phase 5 §II, Phase 6 §XI); now protected. A
high-Inquiry player near the finale may *glimpse* something; the
glimpse is never captioned.

### 4. The tenth fire

Somewhere in the deep wasteland a fire burns that no one tends and no
current cosmology accounts for. It is older than every theory held
about it — older, some say, than the theories' *languages*. Tent-Right
caravans route around it without discussing it. It appears in no
god's function, no faction's doctrine, no Strike's inheritance.

*(Under-text relic: inherited from the world's older strata — see
`Lore/TERMS.md`. In-world, it is simply the under-writing showing
through. That sentence is as much explanation as will ever exist.)*

**Forbidden from answering:** everything and everyone.

### 5. The doll in the wall

In a Choir grove there is a doll, woven into the mycelium where it
should have been eaten, and was not. The Choir keeps some things. It
will not say which, or why — not to the player, not to the Wedded's
own sects, not (as far as anyone can tell) to itself.

*(Under-text relic. The Choir's one unexplained mercy — or inventory,
or bookmark, or wound. Choose your reading; the wall keeps keeping
it.)*

**Forbidden from answering:** the Choir, in every voice it has.

### 6. The Glassblown Remnant

A tiny, silent community that works reversible glass paste and almost
never commits it to fire. They answer to no god; no Strike remembers
them; no substrate claims them; their practices predate every
explanation offered for them, and they have never once corrected
anyone's guess. The shipped faction registry lists them because the
world does — not because anyone knows what they are.

*(Under-text relic — and the resolution of the shipped-content
discrepancy: `Factions.json`'s Glassblown Remnant entry is canon
**as an unexplained remnant**, which is what their name always said.)*

**Forbidden from answering:** all documents; the Remnant themselves
(their silence is constitutional).

### 7. What the Bower sees when she watches

Her resin-cast eyes see — that is canon. *What* seeing is, for a god
of arrangement watching nine centuries of a village rearrange itself
around her — whether she sees objects, or compositions, or futures,
or one enormous unfinished work — is never stated.

**Forbidden from answering:** Seer Liss (who reads her gestures, not
her sight); the Bower herself (whose only speech is arrangement).

### 8. The Gin Frogs

They were here before the lore was. They are in the bestiary, in the
sima-depths, in the corpus's own origin story ("from the tepui idea
and the Gin Frogs…"). No cosmological frame accounts for them and
none will. Some things in a world are just true.

**Forbidden from answering:** everything. Especially anything
tempted to make them load-bearing.

### 9. How many layers down does the writing go (Phase 12)

The Tree scraped a world and wrote over it (Bible §IV, Phase 12). Is
what lies beneath the first page, or the residue of a page scraped so
many times it reads as chaos? Did something write *that*? The canon
commits the question and forecloses every answer: no bleed, relic,
recovered text, god, numen, Spirit, or ending may count the layers,
date them, or bottom them out. *A page scraped often enough looks
blank. It isn't. That is the entire visible truth, forever.*

**Forbidden from answering:** everything, at both tiers — including
design documents. There is no design-canon answer. The regress is
real and unterminated.

## The under-text policy (amended, Phase 12)

**Original clause (Phase 11, preserved for the record):** entries 4–6
canonized three relics from the repo's superseded lore strata as the
world's under-writing, with a hard quota — "the quota is these
three... What makes a relic work is scarcity."

**Amendment (Phase 12, reasoned per this ledger's own rule):** user
direction of 2026-07-15 makes the palimpsest integral, and the
canonization of the spine (`Lore/Design/PALIMPSEST-INTEGRATION.md`
§VII) promotes the under-text from relic-seasoning to **structural
stratum**: the world is canonically an overwriting; bleed-through is
a general phenomenon (the Overwrit is its showcase; small authored
bleed-sites may exist at Tier 3+). The protection therefore
**inverts**: mystery is no longer guarded by *scarcity* of under-text
but by its **permanent illegibility in aggregate.** Hard rules:

1. **No under-text content may ever resolve into a coherent,
   mappable prior world.** Fragments only, forever. The bleeds are
   phenomena, not lore deliveries; no explainer NPC exists or will.
2. **No in-game source may date, name, count, or systematize the
   layers** (see §9).
3. **The named relics (§§4–6) remain individually unexplained**, on
   their original terms.
4. **New bleed-sites are authored one at a time, hand-placed, never
   procedural.** Generated bleeds are wallpaper; wallpaper is
   forbidden.
5. **Recovered under-text yields ambiguity at equal-or-higher
   resolution, never adjudication.** The First Account is the type
   specimen (Bible §IV).
