# How they tell it in Sill

*The sari story, river-village version, as a grandmother tells it on
the dock with the nets across her knees. Every child in Sill can tell
it. No adult in Sill believes it. This is the version the player
grows up on.*

---

Long ago and downriver, when the world was one tall tree — yes, a
tree, don't make that face, this was before faces — the tree got old.
And old things get afraid, same as you at bedtime, and what the tree
was afraid of was forgetting. Because when *you* forget your chores,
the geese go hungry. But when the tree forgot a thing, the thing
stopped being. That's how big it was.

So the tree called seven strong friends, and said: cut me down, and
each of you catch a piece of what I do, and do it after me. And they
came with their one axe between them — seven friends, one axe, that's
how you know it's an old story, nobody shares an axe anymore —

— and six of them swung.

And one of them didn't.

Why didn't he? Ah. That's the question, isn't it. That's the question
the whole world downriver still fights about, and when you're grown
you can fight about it too. Grandmother's too old to fight. Grand-
mother only knows what the story says, and the story says: six swung,
one didn't, the tree fell anyway. Trees do.

And the piece that nobody caught — the little piece, the important
piece, the piece that keeps your name stuck to you like your shadow —
it fell in the river, so to speak. And it's been trying to climb out
ever since. And when it almost climbs out — *almost, almost* — you
can hear it slip back down:

*sari... sari...*

No, it doesn't mean anything. It's the sound a name makes letting go.

And THAT is why — listen now, this is the part that's for you — when
you hear a soft voice down by the water at night saying *sari, sari*,
you don't answer it, and you don't feel sorry for it, and you come
inside. Because it isn't sad. It's *practicing.*

There. Now that's a good story, and none of it's true, and you'll
hear the river all night same as always, saying nothing, saying
nothing, saying nothing.

In you go.
