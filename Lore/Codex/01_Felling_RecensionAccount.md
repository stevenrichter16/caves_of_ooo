# From the Reading: the Felling, collated

*Entered in the Reading at Quillhold. Third recension of the passage;
the two prior hands are preserved beneath in seal. Reader's copy.*

---

Of the Felling itself, four accounts survive that were written within
living memory of it. They agree on the day. They agree on the axe.
They do not agree on the circle.

The account of the First Archive, oldest and most damaged, has it that
seven were summoned and seven came, and that the seventh stood in the
circle and would not strike, and spoke; and what was spoken, the
account does not enter. The hand is Maeleth's own. Where the words of
the seventh should stand, the line is scraped. It is the only scraped
line in her hand that we hold, and she has been asked, and the record
of the asking says: *she wept, and entered nothing.*

The account of the Gantry fragment has it that seven were summoned and
six came.

The account called the Litany of Strikes names each blow and its
inheritance — Memory, Substrate, Preservation, Beauty, Exchange, Roots
— and stops. It does not name a seventh strike. It does not say a
seventh strike was wanted. Those who read absence as testimony make
much of this. We enter their reading beside the others, at its own
weight.

The fourth account is a field-record of sound. It is a list of places
and dates, in a surveyor's hand, marking where the slippage — the
*sari*, as the deep villages say — was first attested. The earliest
marks cluster at the Felling-Site itself, in the first year. They do
not cluster at the Root. Those who hold that the world's un-naming
began with a spoken refusal, in the circle, on the day — they cite
this list. Those who hold otherwise note that a surveyor goes where a
surveyor is sent.

Here the accounts end. What the seventh understood, or intended, or
was — the Reading does not say. I am sorry.

*— entered by the fourth hand of the Quillhold collation, who adds,
against custom and in small letters: the scraped line is the loudest
thing in the archive.*
