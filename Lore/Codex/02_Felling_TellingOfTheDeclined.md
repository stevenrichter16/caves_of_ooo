# The telling of the man who said no

*As told under the cloth at Wellmeet, third night of a guesting,
by a Namer who did not give her name. Written down after, from
memory, by the guest.*

---

You have heard it the way the archives keep it. Sit, there is water,
and hear it the way we keep it.

Seven were called. Not six. Seven. The Tree dreamed into seven
sleepers, and seven woke, and seven walked — from the leaf-cities,
from the river-farms, from wherever their beds were — and seven stood
in the circle at the marked place, where the bark had gone soft as an
old hand.

And the axe went around, and the six took it in their turn, and the
seventh — hear this part; children fidget here, and they are wrong to
— the seventh took the axe too. Held it. Looked a long time at the
marked place.

And said: *No.*

Not ran. Not hid. Not forgot the day, whatever the pretty village
tells its pilgrims. Said no, out loud, in the circle, with the axe in
hand and every reason in the world to swing it. Said no to the Tree
that asked, which is the hardest no there has ever been, because the
Tree had never needed a word for refusal and so had no ear to hear it
with. The Tree went on falling into its plan the way the old and
certain do. The six heard, though. Ask the six what was said, and you
will get six answers — and that, guest, is the part of the telling I
would have you carry: even the gods' memories quarrel. Trust the no.
The words around it were mortal, and mortal words wear out.

Why did he say it? Our line keeps that he *understood* — that some
things cannot be handed over, only done, and a god of the
handed-over-thing would be a lie wearing a crown. That is what we
keep. I will not tell you we know it. Knowing is the archive's word.
We are not the archive. We keep it the way you keep a fire: not
because you can prove the cold, but because you have felt it.

They blamed him before the Tree hit the ground, and they have blamed
him for a thousand years, and blame is also a way of remembering.
Now the water. You have listened well.
