# Internal memorandum: the founding provisions

*Saccharine Concord internal circular, unsigned, suppressed at Tally
within the season of its writing. Copies persist. Copies always
persist; we are a documentary people; it is one of our costs.*

---

Colleagues.

You have all handled the founding ledgers. You have all admired the
first hand — his hand, before the exhaustion got into the
downstrokes. This circular concerns entry forty-one.

Entry forty-one is the provisioning of the ritual party, year zero.
It is a clean entry. It is, in fact, the cleanest entry in the book,
made before the world's accounts went strange. It records: waybread,
water, rope, oil, one axe (one), and bedding.

Bedding for six.

Not seven. Six. Waybread in six portions. Six bedrolls against the
cold at the marked place. The provisioner of the age did not
misprovision; read any other entry in his hand and you will find a
man who counted the world as it stood. Either a seventh was expected
and given no bed and no bread — for a walk of eleven days — or.

We do not file conclusions. We file counts. The count is six.

Those of you who have paid the meeting-fee and stood at the high desk
will know that the Counter, asked about entry forty-one, neither
confirms nor denies, and that his face while not-answering is the
same face he keeps for debts he has personally decided never to
collect. Draw no conclusion from this. Draw no conclusion from
anything in this circular. This circular does not exist; the copies
you are holding are a rounding error.

We note in closing that the story of a seventh who *refused* is
worth, at current rates, a great deal — pilgrim traffic, archive
fees, the whole economy of blame — and that a story of a senile Tree
that *miscounted* is worth nothing at all to anyone, except that it
would be the only version in which nobody, anywhere, is owed an
apology. We are traders. We know what people buy. It is not always
what happened.

Balance to you and yours.
