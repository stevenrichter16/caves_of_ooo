# The dimming song

*Sung in the middle-depth villages at the Sealed Dim, when the lamps
are shuttered wall by wall. Children learn it as a counting game
before they learn what the counting is for. The tune is small and
goes down at the end of every line.*

---

One dim for the doorway,
shut it soft, shut it soft.
Two dims for the passage
where the water talks.

Three dims for the far wall —
kiss the wall goodnight.
Grandmother is in it,
she will hold the light.

Say your name to no one.
Hold it in your hand.
Names go walking, walking,
down the dark, and

*(here the children hush, because the next part is whispered)*

if a soft voice asks you,
asks you nice, asks you twice,
sweet as sugar-water:
*"who is there? who is there?"* —

you are stone, you are moss,
you are nobody's, you are the wall's.
Morning knows your name, love.
Morning gives it back.

*(and the last line is spoken, not sung, by the oldest voice in the
room)*

Sleep now. The patch is watching. It has never once slept, and it
loves you, and the door is shut.
