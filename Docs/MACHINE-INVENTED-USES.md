# Invented uses for the machines that had none

Provenance: user direction, 2026-11-19: "if a use for a machine does not exist, analyze the game and the purpose of the machine and invent a use for that machine that flows naturally with the rest of the game mechanics and the lore", and "you have full free reign of the game".

This replaces the dossiers' standing advice ([MachineDesign/](MachineDesign/README.md): "no demonstrated purpose, do not build") for the machines the owner named. It does **not** relax the principles in [SYSTEMS-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES.md](SYSTEMS-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES.md): finite accounting, custody and save integrity, honest unsupported-state limits, protected lore, additive saves, and the ordinary path (repair, healing, carrying) staying free. Evidence tags: **[V]** read in code or data this session; **[V\*]** verified by a dossier at its time; **[I]** inference or design, to be checked by the slice's first RED test or a lore read.

## 1. What the game gives a machine to attach to

A machine has an honest purpose when it lets the player do something through a rule the game already runs. The research found five such rules.

1. **One rule engine for energy and coatings.** A cell can hold heat, cold and charge (0..2) plus coatings (oil, water, ice) and residues; `Reactions.json` turns them into outcomes (oil ignites on heat, water steams, ice melts, water conducts charge). Every fire and lightning ability enters through `ZoneTileStateSystem` (`ApplyFireToTile`, `AddCharge`) and `ResolveAfterAbility`. [V] Machines that put a bounded, previewed amount of energy into a cell inherit every reaction, including ones nobody authored for them.
2. **One ignition seam.** `IgnitionSources.Find` answers "is there a flame in reach" for lighting a torch and relighting a finite cooking fire, and `GroveLaw.OnIgnite` charges the grove law where every ignition passes. [V] Anything that makes fire should be one more answer to that lookup, not a new verb.
3. **Concerns.** Twenty settlement problems, each with two or three approaches (Supply, Confront, Read, Restore, Settle) that set place conditions. [V] Several ask for goods the player can only buy or loot today: lamp oil (Quillhold, Slip), ward oil (the Quiet's Door), fire clay (Sill's oven, Cinderhold's seal), iron spikes, an oak haft and a binding for Cinderhold's pump. A machine earns a place by being one more honest way to get such a good, with its own cost.
4. **No survival clock.** There is no thirst and no ambient cold; the only exposure is the Beating glare, and a hat answers it. [V\*] So a machine cannot be a relief from a need. It must be an opportunity (a new thing to reach or make), an alternative with different costs, or a preparation for a known hazard.
5. **Site-bound and carried fixtures.** The Wellmeet main well shows a site-owned part with readable states; the four camp kits show a carried tool that becomes a station when set down. [V] Both patterns are reusable.

Rules the inventions below follow: attach to an existing rule or loop; add a path, never take one away; spend something finite or place-bound and never mint money (the Beating's salt economy was already inflated once by a free salt vein; EC5); preview the consequence; keep canon nouns plain and gear-only where a body could be mistaken for a specimen.

## 3. The first build: the flint striker (spark)

**The gap.** `TorchLightPart` lights "at nearby fire" and the camp hearth relights "from flame". A player with no flame, no mutation and a dead torch had no way to make fire. The hearth kit's "carry a flame from a village fire" was a deliberate consequence, kept: a striker does not erase it, it gives a second approach with its own cost (finite strikes, a spike to renew).

**What it is.** `FlintStrikerPart` and the `FlintStriker` blueprint (20 strikes, weight 1, sold by the Tinker). It is not a fire, a stack or a light source.

| Behaviour | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Lights a torch or relights a finite fire with no flame in reach | `IgnitionSources.Find` returns a carried, dry striker with a strike left, only after real fires and equipped lit torches; `IgnitionSources.Consume` spends one strike inside the caller's transaction after the claim, undone with it. The torch and hearth verbs are unchanged. |
| A real fire in reach is always used first and costs nothing | Search order. |
| Strikes a spark into an adjacent cell | Rows "strike a spark <direction>" appear only where `TileReactionSystem.HeatWouldIgnite` says one point of heat would light a combustible coating; it writes heat through `ZoneTileStateSystem.ApplyFireToTile` and resolves, after the action commits, so an outer failure never leaves a fire with the strike returned. |
| Will not spark wet or frozen | Same thresholds as the torch. |
| Renewed by spending an iron spike | "knap a new edge": one spike, ten strikes, never above the maximum; the reclamation chain's spikes are the sink. Disabled row says why when there is no spike. |
| Reader, save, diagnostics | `IDescribeState` line ("7 strikes left"); two public fields round-trip; `StrikerLit`, `StrikerSpark`, `StrikerKnapped`, `StrikerRejected` in the `event` category. |

**Consequences.** A spark into a slick burns it through the shared reaction, whose row also damages occupants of that cell; the row warns when you are standing in oil yourself. Whatever the world does for any fire ability happens here too, because the heat is written by the same routine. No rule was written or bypassed in the striker, and the grove law is **not** claimed: it is charged where an entity ignites through the thermal seam, and a bare burning slick in a grove was not tested.

**Honest limits.** Voice and art: the description is a mechanical placeholder and the item is a glyph (`/`). No tinder or dry-fuel model exists, so the striker lights only things the game already treats as ignitable. Only heat is built; the charge rod is design.

## 3b. The second build: rest beside the hearth

**The gap.** A rest is an instant full heal plus a 60-tick clock jump, free at any waysite campfire. The camp hearth was kept from resting ("the shipped coals opt out of rest"; "rest at a lit hearth" was listed as an open balance decision) because nothing simulates a zone-bound fire through a clock jump: a finite fire would keep its fuel through a night.

**The invention.** `HearthRestPart` on the hearth: the fire burns, while you sleep, what it would have burned in those ticks: ticks times its own burn rate, rounded up (60 ticks at the hearth's 0.25 is 15 fuel, about a timber and a half). The row says so ("rest (the fire burns about 15 fuel)"). It is refused up front, with the numbers in the reason, when the fire is out or has less than that, and nothing changes on a refusal. "Rest until the next time of day" is not refused for being longer than the fire can last: the fire burns what it can (about 15 fuel over its roughly 60 ticks of life, by the thermal part's own cooling), goes out in the night, and the row says so ("burns about 15 fuel, then goes out"); late in a band, when the fire does last to morning, it says how little it burns. Both rows share one rule: the fire must be able to keep at least a normal night. (A first version required the fire to last the whole night, which made the row unavailable for most of every 300-tick band; found in play 2026-11-19 and fixed.) Heal, bleeding cure, clock advance and the hostile-within-eight rule are the shared `RestSystem`; fuel is charged only after it has admitted the rest, and returns with the transaction. The fire also cools through the night by the thermal part's own per-turn decay, and the rest is refused ("relight it from a flame first") if it would fall below cooking heat before the rest ends, so one lighting is about one night and the next night needs a flame again: this is the striker's nightly job. A fire that burns out in the night is cold and dark when you wake.

**Why it flows.** It uses the hearth's own numbers and no new constant; supplies the cost the owner asked a rest-at-fire to have (finite timber you carry or stash in the camp box); keeps every other fire's rest exactly as it was (a separate Part with its own commands, so the free waysite rest and the coals' opt-out are untouched); and answers the documented limit by charging the fuel instead of pretending the fire was simulated. A camp becomes a place you can sleep, at the price of the timber you brought.

**Honest limits.** A night is a full heal for about 15 fuel: a real, bounded lever that the owner should be able to retune by changing the hearth's `BurnRate`, `MaxFuel` or the timber's fuel. Other fuel burners (a torch in the pack) still do not burn through a rest. No Editor play yet.

## 3c. The third build: settling at the still

**The gap.** The still gate asks for "a difficult local feed into a useful liquid", with feed accounting, bounded processing, fuel and an output; the camp still only brews reagents into effect flasks. The Sodden's bog-mire is exactly a difficult local feed: acid, viscous, drawn into flasks by the shipped transfers, and good for nothing else. [V]

**The invention.** At a standing still (communal or set-down), a flask of bog-mire settles to **clean water or oil, four units to one**, chosen at the still, over a lit fire within two cells that spends **5 of its own fuel** per batch. The two outputs compete for one finite feed; the dregs are spent; output times four never exceeds the feed (a 11-unit flask is two batches of four and 3 dregs). Rows say the numbers ("settle 12 mire to 3 water") and refuse up front, with the numbers, when there is no fire, when no fire can spare 5 fuel, or when the flask holds fewer than 4. A carried still is a kit and offers nothing.

**Why it flows.** It is the roster's own picture ("a swamp workshop and a desert workshop could use the same machine for very different purposes"): the Sodden gets water or oil out of mire, other places get nothing from it. Fire is coupled, not assumed: the hearth, or any burning fire with fuel, pays for the work, so the still and the hearth become one workshop. The oil is real: it burns by the shared reactions when poured and sparked with a flint striker (the same chain the Peat Works already uses for rendering). It is the rendering the peat-cutters already do, made portable.

**Deliberately not done.** Brine is not settled: a free salt source would inflate the money the Beating's salt economy rests on (EC5). Water has no thirst consumer today (irrigation and flasks only), so the water branch is modest; oil is the branch with the fire loop.

**Honest limits.** Voice review of the strings; no Editor play; flasks only (no standing tank); the fire's fuel is spent per batch rather than per elapsed time, as for rest.

## 3d. Lighting, fuel and glow from the menus (2026-11-19, from play)

Asked after playing the kits: lighting should not need the pack, a fire should always say its fuel in turns, and a lit fire should look lit.

- **Light from the C menu.** `FireStarting.AddCellRows` adds a "light it" row to the interact menu (and look-mode menu) of a cell beside you where one point of heat would ignite a combustible coating (an oil slick, which is a tile coating with no object to hang a row on): "light it with the torch" when you hold a burning torch (free, preferred) or "light it with the flint striker (1 strike)"; a wet or worn striker shows a disabled row that says why. The row names the carried item that performs it (`InventoryAction.Via`), so the world menu runs the same command the pack menu runs. Fires and ground torches already lit from their own menus through `IgnitionSources`; an out fire now shows a disabled relight row saying what stops it (no fuel, or no flame: a burning fire beside you, a lit torch, or a striker).
- **Fuel and turns, always.** `CellStatusReadout.FuelLine` feeds the C menu's status block, look mode and (as an `IDescribeState` on `FuelPart`) both examine readers: "Fuel: 20 left, about 80 turns once you light it." A burning finite fire (the hearth) also says how long its heat lasts, because a lighting cools below cooking heat before a full load of fuel is gone: "Burning for about 60 more turns before it needs a flame again."
- **Glow.** The hearth's light is a campfire's (radius 8, intensity 0.8, with its flicker), it turns on the moment a relight commits instead of at the next turn's end, and a burning hearth is drawn in fire colors while an unlit one stays dark. Not verified in the Editor: I could not play it.

## 3e. Quality of life for the camp (2026-11-19, from play)

Asked after playing the kits: sixteen small things that make the camp easier to run without changing what any of it costs or does. Two were set aside on purpose (stations drawing straight from the camp box, and banking a fire).

- **One press does the chore.** `feed to full` adds as many timber as fit; `stoke` feeds and lights in one action (one strike if a striker lights it); `settle all` runs every settleable flask; the hearth rows have hotkeys (f, u, l, t). Each is one transaction, so any refusal anywhere gives the timber, strikes and fuel back.
- **Say what it does.** Every row of the camp family carries a one-line `Hint` (`InventoryAction.Hint`, presentation only: no rule reads it). The world menu reserves up to three small lines under its list and shows the highlighted row's hint ("Stoke: adds all the timber the fire will take, then lights it from a flame you carry, in one action."); the pack menu shows it on its detail line. A menu with no hints keeps its old shape. Rest rows name the time of day you wake in ("rest until Dusk") and what the rest heals and burns.
- **Strikes shown wherever the striker shows.** The name reads "flint striker [17]", relight and light rows say "(1 strike, 17 left)", and a worn flint says "knap an edge" where a flame was wanted. A light row beside a camp kit names it ("next to the camp box"), so an oil slick is not lit by accident.
- **Kit verbs.** `make camp here (N kits)` lays out every carried kit in one action (fire first, the still within reach of it, the bench, the box away from the flames; a kit with no legal cell stays in the pack and the message says so). `pack up camp (N things)` takes up everything of yours within 4 cells, all or nothing. `move` is one row that opens a cell picker: the cursor starts on the kit and moves with the arrow keys (or the mouse) within two cells of you, its border green where the kit fits and red where it does not (the colour is the same `HeldFixtureActions.CanMoveTo` the move runs, so it is never a promise the move breaks); Enter on green moves it as one action, Enter on red says why, Escape leaves. The kit keeps its ID, fuel, contents and camp name (a burning fire goes out, as in taking it up); a kit too heavy to lift shows the row disabled with the reason. "Where it fits" hints appear on set-down rows ("(by your fire)").
- **A camp has a name.** `name this camp` cycles Home, Peat edge, Dig site, Lookout, Cache, Workshop, Field camp, Waystop, then clears. The name is stored on every kit of the camp (the same eight-cell cluster the notes already use) and shown in the arrival and map notes; nothing reads it. Free, undone with the rest of the transaction.
- **What you left.** The arrival and map notes end with "As you left it: hearth lit, 12 fuel, box holds 3." Quiet when there is nothing to say (an empty box, a bare hearth), so the old wording is exact for those.
- **What can I do here?** Examining a fire says what its rows would offer you now ("Here you can: feed it (3 timber), light it, dry 1 wet thing."), a hearth says what a night costs ("You could rest here: it burns about 15 fuel."), and a still says what your mire would settle to or what it is missing. Each Part describes its own affordance, so a new kit that carries those Parts gets the line without new code.

- **A lit fire is not offered a light.** The relight and stoke rows appear only for a fire that is out (cold, or out of fuel); a fire at cooking heat or above shows neither, so nothing offers to spend a strike on a flame (a lit torch already offered "extinguish" instead of "light"). Feeding a burning fire is unchanged. A fire that is lit but too low to last the night says to light it again once it dies down.
- **The still's menu shows the mix.** Opening a still (communal or set-down) shows, above its rows, what is in the mix, what it would make (name, each effect with its potency, the form), why a mix makes nothing, and how many a full batch brews: the same preview the pack's Crafting panel draws (`BrewMenuReadout` over `BrewingService.PreviewBrew`). The menu reopens after each pick, so the lines change as reagents are added. The brew rows describe themselves and the batch row names its size. The seam is `IMenuStatus`, a Part-level hook for any station that wants lines in its menu.
- **Try it without the walk.** The Home scenario menu has two new entries that start the fresh game on ordinary wild ground (the nearest Spread zone the camp-kit rules accept, found from the world map at start): "Camp Kits - wild ground", and "wild ground, night, hurt" (the clock at the start of the Dark band, health at two fifths) for the "I need a camp" case. The request is one-shot (`ScenarioRunner.PendingWildStart`) and a continued save never reads it; the scene's own start setting still wins.

**Honest limits.** No Editor play of any of this: the menu's hint block is exercised through its state (rows reserved, highlighted hint) and not through a rendered frame. "As you left it" is a read of the stored zone, which is not simulated while you are away. A camp name is a label, not a place the game knows about.

## 3f. The fourth build: the Cinderhold mine pump (2026-11-19)

**The gap.** The Drowned Level has a Restore approach on a repair site named "Pump" with real materials (iron spikes, an oak haft, a binding; or fire clay for a patch that fails after 600 turns), and the owner said "places have no generated object for a weir or a pump yet" so it was repaired only by talking. A machine that exists only as a conversation line is not a machine.

**The invention.** `PlaceSiteObjectPart` is a generic body for any data-defined repair site, and `MinePump` is the first. It owns nothing: the state lives where it always has (`SettlementManager`'s `RepairableSiteState`), the rules are one function (`PlaceSiteRepairs.Check`, which `RepairPlaceSite` now also uses), and the repair is the same call the elder's "[Repair the pump.]" makes. So there are two doors to one room: nothing is taken away, and a Restore approach finishes the same way whichever door was used.

What the object adds is the world's side. It is placed fresh-only beside Cinderhold's well through the existing installation placement (a row with a `Site`, kept out of the every-town machine census because it is the town's own). It reads as "mine pump (sheared)", "(patched)" or "(running)" in its own name and colour; examining it says what became of it (the authored description, how long a patch will hold, and in the full reader what you could do from what you carry); its interact menu shows the state and, for each repair, the materials with what you carry ("iron spike 1/2, oak haft 0/1, ..."); it offers "mend the mine pump for good" and "patch the mine pump for now" when they could be made and shows them disabled with exactly what is missing when they could not; and the repair is one action done inside the action's transaction with the pack and the site captured first, so a refusal, an outer failure or a listener that throws puts the materials and the site back (the announcement the repair shows is the only thing not taken back).

**Why it flows.** It makes the Restore approach a thing you do in a place, with your hands, rather than a menu choice; it uses no new currency and mints nothing; the clay patch still fails on its own clock and the lasting mend still needs the reclamation chain's parts, which is that chain's purpose.

**Deliberately not done.** No flooded level and no water that the pump moves: the dossier's finding stands that nothing in the zone is submerged, so the object makes the repair tangible and readable without pretending to a fluid simulation. The portable second pump and the supply-a-still uses wait for a bulk-liquid consumer. No new art (a glyph, like the forge before its sprite); wording is placeholder pending voice review.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. The object appears in new Cinderhold zones only (a town already generated and cached keeps what it has, as for every installation). The generic part is ready for the weir, scales and vats, but each needs its own placement row and a read of where it stands.

## 3g. The fifth build: the sounding rod (2026-11-20)

**The gap.** The map deliberately does not show where the ground is hollow, and in a deep cave the only way to learn what lies beyond the next wall is to dig or walk. The dossier ([MachineDesign/machine-resonator.md](MachineDesign/machine-resonator.md)) found that the pitched discovery (a buried structure to the northeast) has no target in this world, and that the honest first instrument hears only what the world really hides: unseen geometry, stairs beyond sight, and the seams inside the mine-gallery rooms.

**The invention.** The sounding rod, a carried iron-shod rod sold by Tinkers (30% of stock), driven into the floor of an ordinary deep cave and struck: one action. The stone answers about ground you have not seen on this floor and only three kinds of it: **open ground** (a stretch of at least six connected unseen cells), **a bore** (the nearest unseen stair; the rod cannot tell up from down, which is the leftover exploration and also a retreat lead) and **dense stone** (the nearest unseen mineral vein, never named). Each is a bearing in eight points and a distance in three bands (close, middling, far), at least four paces away, never a count, coordinate or name. It hears terrain only: no creatures, items, containers or inscriptions.

**The cost is noise.** The strike rings through the stone (`Noise.Mark`), which is how eyeless sound-hunters (the Pale Stalkers of the deeper tiers) find you; it consumes nothing, so the price of knowing is exposure, not a resource. How far the stone carries depends on what it is, from the depth table the game already has: sandstone 12 paces, limestone 18, shale 14, slate 20, quartzite 26, obsidian 30.

**What it will not do.** It refuses on the surface ("too loose to carry a sounding"), and in any column the game keeps unmapped (villages, sinkhole floors, the authored columns, and the Gleaner's Cellar, which the layout plan would otherwise call ordinary) with the same words as for ground it cannot bite into ("no solid floor here"), so a refusal never marks a floor as special. A dropped rod offers only pickup. It never reads another floor or the world map, so sinkhole mouths and the sealed library stay unmapped.

**Quality of life.** The row names how far the stone carries here ("sound the ground (carries about 18 paces)"); on the surface or in a refused column it is shown disabled with the reason; the rod's reader says how far it would carry now and what it last heard and how long ago; the reading is kept on the rod and saved with it; an interrupted action neither rings nor records.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. The ground table's numbers are proposals, tunable. Only one tool (the pitch's "recognizable signature" needs a buried-feature layer the world lacks). Wording is placeholder pending voice review. A Tinker already generated and cached has no rod; an unvisited town gets it on first visit.

## 3h. The sixth build: the steeping trough (2026-11-20)

**The gap.** Acid is the one hazard near the start that is both dangerous and under-served: a Marlback Soursprayer's spray is a damage-over-time that costs about 37 HP unresisted and about 18 with a +35 resistance, and the only counters were buying a particular apron or eating a particular meal. The bath dossier ([MachineDesign/machine-bath.md](MachineDesign/machine-bath.md)) found the honest V1: a small, optional, gear-only preparation that makes a garment turn acid for a while, and nothing more (the roster's other half, shedding spores, has no state to act on).

**The invention.** The steeping trough, a camp kit like the bench and still (set it down, steep, pick it up; a Tinker may stock one, 20%). Beside it, a carried, unworn, unstacked garment of leather or cloth can be steeped with **one pitchpod resin and one fire clay**: it then gives **+35 acid resistance for 100 turns of wear** (`EnhancementSteeped`). The reagents are used up whether or not acid is ever met; there is no refund and no byproduct; the garment is not consumed; steeping again replaces what is left (the row says "replaces 37 turns"); a piece that already holds two treatments refuses a third.

**How the wear works.** The treatment lives on the garment and travels with it (carried, sold, dropped, saved). Putting the garment on gives the wearer a `SteepedAcidEffect` for the turns that remain; the effect counts down on the wearer's own turns (so world-map travel does not wear it); taking the garment off keeps what is left (paused, not lost); when the turns run out the garment is plain again and the wearer is told. Two steeped garments worn together each give their own. Acid still does at least 1 damage per hit until your resistance reaches 100 (full immunity); the reader says so. The garment's remaining turns follow the wearer's turns while it is worn, so its reader is right and a re-equip never refills it.

**Gear only, and the word.** The trough offers rows for garments by Part allow-list (armor, organic or cloth material, takeable, not a creature), so a person, body or specimen can never qualify; its word is "steep". "Bath" belongs to the Curation and the Bower and never appears in what the player reads.

**Quality of life.** Each row names the garment and what it replaces; its hint states the reagents, the effect and that wear pauses when the garment is taken off; with a reagent missing the row is disabled and says which; with nothing to steep one disabled row says what fits; the trough's reader says how many garments you carry could be steeped and what reagents you carry; the garment's reader says how many turns of wear remain.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. The numbers (35, 100 turns) are the dossier's proposal. No new art (a glyph). The reagents are bought or found; nothing makes pitchpod resin or fire clay yet (the firing build, which fires clay, would make the second). Wording is placeholder pending voice review.

## 3i. The seventh build: a body for every repair site (2026-11-20)

## 3j. The eighth build: baking clay at the still (2026-11-20)

**The gap.** Fire clay has one source in the shipped world: the Morrowfast mender's four. Yet the pump's patch asks two, the Cinderhold "seal" approach four, three repairs list it as a material, and the steeping trough one per garment. A thermal machine's pitch ("fire clay has no maker") had no raw material: there is no clay deposit to dig.

**The invention.** The Sodden's bog-mire, the difficult local feed the still already settles into water or oil, is silt and acid water: boil the water off and what is left bakes into clay. At a set-down or communal still beside a lit fire, a flask holding eight or more mire offers **"bake N mire to 1 fire clay"**: eight mire and ten of the fire's fuel make one fire clay (a flask holds twelve, so at most one per flask; the rest stays as mire). It competes with settling for the same finite feed, and the output never exceeds it (one clay per eight mire; test-pinned).

**Why it is not a money tap.** Fire clay is worth 1 dram. It is a repair and quest material, so its value is what it opens (a pump patched, a seal plugged, a garment steeped), not what it sells for; and the fuel and the carrying of mire are the real costs.

**Quality of life.** The row names the amount it takes and the product ("bake 8 of the 12 mire to 1 fire clay") and says in its hint what the fire spends; a missing fire or too little fuel shows it disabled with the reason ("burns 10 and the fullest has 9"); the still's reader offers the bake next to the settle; an interrupted action gives back the mire, the fuel and the clay; a second clay merges into the first stack; no room for the clay refuses before anything is spent.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. The ratio (8 mire, 10 fuel) is a proposal. The product is the existing fire clay (no new item, no art). It needs the camp's hands (the content factory wired at bootstrap).

## 3k. The ninth build: the Concord's carriage (2026-11-20)

**What it is.** At a Concord post that keeps a ledger (the Factor at Cinderhold's pruning post, the Envoy at the Last Counter) the greeting now offers "[Carriage] Send a parcel to the other post (about 3 days)". Choosing it lists the stacks in the pack the cart will take, heaviest first with the fee beside each; choosing a stack shows the whole bargain (days, your purse, and the rows "all", "half" and "one" for a stack, each with its own fee, only the ones you can pay). Choosing a row seals the goods in your name, takes the fee, and returns to the greeting, which now reads what is on the cart. At the other post the greeting offers "[Carriage] Collect what has arrived (N)" once the days have passed, and hands the goods back.

**Why it flows.** `Lore/Codex/06_ConcordCarriageContract.md` writes the contract: whole, dry and unopened for the rate agreed at the desk; delivery guaranteed to the Last Counter and no further; no unboxed spores, no bodies not sealed to Curation standard, nothing that sings. The send node quotes that last limit where it applies ("The cart stops at the Last Counter"), and the refusals use the clause's own words. The posts are the two authored Concord places whose profiles keep a ledger today (`PruningPost`, `ConcordPost`); Tally (`CentralExchange`) is the seat but keeps no ledger yet, so it has no cart. Adding it is one row in `PostProfiles` once a keeper stands there.

**Why it is honest.**
- The goods are the real entity, held in a saved `ConcordCarriagePart` on the player and nowhere else: every stack count, flask volume and name survives the road (a save round-trip test pins it). A partial send splits the stack with the game's own `SplitStack` and the arrival merges back through `AddObject`, so nothing is copied or lost, and a stack that will not divide is refused whole.
- The fee leaves the purse through `Ledger.TryDebit` (a sink): 2 drams plus 1 per 5 weight, so a round trip costs money and mints none (a test asserts no credit enters).
- Arrival is the clock compared at the destination (a day for every four map cells, three days between the two posts), never a timer on a zone that is not loaded.
- The cart runs on the same ledger as credit: a name in default or with standing too low to be offered credit has no room on the cart, and the node says so. At most six parcels ride at once.
- It refuses what cannot change hands (`TradeSystem.CanBeTraded`, so a rental, a bound cargo or a quest item vetoes exactly as at any counter), worn items, items held for work in progress, and clause five by what a thing *is*, not how its blueprint is spelled: a tag (`Spore`, `Corpse`, `Sings`) opts any future item in, and named sets cover what exists today (the two spore items, the three unsealed bodies, and the Choir's living cutting as the thing that sings). A sword called a sporeblade is a sword. A keeper who is stunned, frozen or on fire cannot see to the cart, as at any counter; a ledger found late (a day missed while resting) is found by the cart's own check, as Borrow and Repay find it.
- The ordinary path (carry it yourself) is unchanged and free.

**Quality of life.** The send list is sorted heaviest first and paged eight to a page ("[More goods...]", "[Earlier goods...]"), so every stack can be reached; every row names its fee; the bargain is shown before anything is spent and rows the purse cannot pay are not offered; a partial send (all, half, one, each offered once, and only when the stack can be divided) saves splitting the stack by hand; the greeting lists each parcel with its place and time ("due in 3 days", "ready to collect", "waiting at the Last Counter"); opening the world map adds one line ("The Concord's cart carries 2 parcels in your name; 1 has arrived at the Last Counter"); a collection that does not fit the pack leaves the parcel on the cart and says so; a refusal always changes nothing.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. The rate (2 + 1 per 5 weight), the road speed (a day per four cells), the six-parcel cap and the page size are proposals; the choice of the Choir's cutting as the thing that sings is an inference from the contract's wording. Clause five's "anything the Bower-Folk have asked after twice" cannot be evaluated and is not enforced. There is no recall: once sealed, the parcel goes where it was sent (the fee is kept, as clause the first has it). Cached towns keep their old keepers and conversations (the cart is added in conversation, not in the keeper's authored tree, so it appears for every living keeper at those two zones, including saved ones).

## 3l. The tenth build: a clay-wash against fire at the trough (2026-11-20)

**What it is.** The steeping trough (3h) gave a garment a finite acid-proofing. Now it also gives a **clay-wash against fire**: 2 fire clay for +35 heat resistance for 100 turns of wear, the same shape as the steeping, on the same garments. The still bakes fire clay from bog-mire (3j), so the chain is bog-mire, hearth fuel, fire clay, garment: three machines that were built apart now feed one another. Burning is a real hazard (a burning tick is heat damage, which `HeatResistance` reduces by the same rule acid uses, never below 1 until resistance reaches 100).

**How.** `EnhancementSteeped` and `SteepedAcidEffect` gained a `Kind` ("acid" by default, so every earlier save loads as it always was; "fire" for the clay-wash) and the effect remembers the stat it shifted, so removal reverts exactly what was added. A garment holds one treatment or the other: a new one replaces it, and the row says which ("replaces 41 turns of steeping"). The trough offers "clay-wash X" rows only once you carry some fire clay (a chain you have started), for the first three garments, and a disabled row says what it lacks ("You need 2 fire clay; you carry 1"). The command is the steeping command with a `|fire` suffix; anything else is refused. An interrupted action gives back both clay and restores the prior treatment with its kind.

**Quality of life.** The reader names both recipes and what you carry; row hints state the cost and the effect; the message on success says which proofing the garment now has; turns are spent only while the garment is worn and paused when it comes off, as with the steeping.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. Amount (35), turns (100) and cost (2 clay) are proposals. It does not make a wearer fireproof (a hit still does at least 1 damage), and it does not touch the Beating glare.

## 3m. The eleventh build: water puts your hearth out (2026-11-20)

**The gap.** Pouring water onto your own lit hearth wrote the water to the ground and left the fire burning (a probe showed the hearth at the same temperature, the same fuel, and the log line "You pour 6 of water onto the ground."). That is the plainest common-sense interaction there is with no supported path.

**The rule.** `FireDouse` runs after a pour commits (the same place the puddle's contact effects run). If the cell holds a lit hearth that *you set down* (a Player-origin, installed fixture; a town's hearth is not yours to put out) and the liquid is one that does not burn (`Combustibility <= 0`: water, bog mire, brine), the hearth takes a cold dose through the existing `ApplyHeat` event, 40 degrees per unit poured, scaled by its heat capacity. The fire's own thermal rules do the rest: it crosses below cooking heat (150) and goes dark (the glow syncs at once), or it only burns lower. A fire at 450 needs about eight units, so a full flask puts it out and one splash only cools it; a fiercer fire takes more. The timber is not burned by being doused: the hearth keeps its fuel and can be lit again from a flame by the ordinary verbs. Oil and pitch never douse.

**Why it is not "banking".** The owner excluded "banking the fire" (managing embers) from the camp list. This does not manage anything: it is the one physical consequence a player already expects when water meets fire, applied through the dose the frost abilities use, and it spends the water.

**Quality of life.** The pour rows over your lit hearth carry a hint ("Puts out your hearth. It keeps its fuel." or "Cools your hearth; it burns lower."); other cells' rows are unchanged; the message says which happened; a diag record (`furniture/HearthDoused`) carries before and after.

**Honest limits.** Not played in the Editor. 40 degrees a unit is a proposal. Only fixtures you set down are doused; the ordinary wild campfire and a town's hearth are not touched. It does not make steam or put out other burning things (a burning creature already has its own rules).

## 5. Considered and not built (2026-11-20)

Written so the next pass does not repeat the reading. Each line says what blocks it and what would unblock it.

- **A ledger keeper at Tally** (would give the cart a third post). The installation framework refuses creatures by design (`InstallationRegistry.AdmissionIssue`), Tally's composition validator rejects creatures, and service residents come from `VillagePopulationBuilder`'s role list. - **A letter of credit** (drams moved between posts). Drams weigh nothing and nothing takes them, so there is no reason to move them.
