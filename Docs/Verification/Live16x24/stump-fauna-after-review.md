## Wet-frog contrast decision

In Gorge08, one blue frog is visible on the green water and another on pink-stone
land. Their dark boundary and blue mass separate them from both backgrounds at
this normal gameplay scale. They are small, but not lost in the field. Thus this
review finds **no named blocking contrast failure requiring a redraw now**.

The cue materially compresses the pale-versus-green distinction between the two
species. Neutral portraits09/10 recover those source palettes and are visibly
different; their names and state rows provide additional precise identity. That
is a bounded acceptance, not permission to treat a palette-flattening wet cue as
finished visual polish. Carry forward **wet-state species differentiation and
small-frog readability** as explicit issues for the serious area-by-area pass.
Do not remove the real wet cue merely to make source colors brighter, and do not
invent new atlas art without a demonstrated specific failure.

submitted status cue; the reader illustrates the same source body neutrally while

claimed equality of world/portrait tint. No transient hurt cue is claimed visible
in these still images.
