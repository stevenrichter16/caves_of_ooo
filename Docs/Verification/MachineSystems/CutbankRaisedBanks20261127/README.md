# Cutbank Crossing has a solid face, a wet opening and two dry detours

An ordinary seed-65 traveler left Brine Camp at 42 HP, walked to
the Dressing Shelter, and crossed the eastern edge into Cutbank
Crossing. Each comparison began at the west fork (20,12) at tick
7230 with 42 HP and no active effects. The regional clock showed
rain during these routes.

## Peat face and opening

Look selected the actual peat bank at (26,10), showed its solid
flag and 10 HP, and offered its Examine action. Standing west of
that face, one ordinary directional input left the player in place.
The attack dealt three damage and the bank stayed solid. Sari-snakes
interrupted the return from the face. The direct opening was checked
from a fresh copy of the fork.

Look selected the adjacent live mire and Examine explained its acid
and agility effects. The first step applied bog mire and cost 1 HP.
Six east steps put the traveler in the gap at (26,12) at tick 7290
with 36 HP. Both following sari-snakes also acquired the coating.
This playtest covered the gap at the western face. The full wet
crossing was left untested.

## Dry detours

The north boards and south bank each covered 58 ordinary steps to
the east fork at (60,12), tick 7810. Both routes kept the traveler
out of the mire. Sari-snakes caused injuries on both journeys. The
north boards crossed the watcher’s marked ground and drew a warning.
No watcher damage was observed. The south bank stayed outside the
claim. Fresh Continue restored the northern branch's destination,
18 HP, exact terrain and absence of mire coating.

The west notice describes the two detours but omits the watched part
of the north boards. The passage post suggests the longer boardwalks
are open, although the actual claim overlaps the north boards. Those
readers now distinguish the watched north stretch from the free
south bank in the [reader follow-up](../CutbankRouteReaders20261127/README.md).
The solid peat and mire already give the player usable visible
boundaries. This playtest ran on the existing terrain.
