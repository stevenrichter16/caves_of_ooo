# Combat decisions after the item-utility tranche

Read-only source research, 2026-10-08. CoO checkout inspected at `f239fb81b`; primary reference is the available full local `/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project`, not the smaller repository copy. No production code changed. This is a proposal, not a statement that any new behavior has shipped or that the inspected Qud source matches its newest release.

## What the comparison actually establishes

Qud separates offensive, defensive and retreat opportunities and lets relevant parts offer commands. Its command entries can invoke real inventory actions. This is not a perfect global tactical optimizer: `IAICommandListEvent.Add` duplicates candidates by priority, and `AICommandList.HandleCommandList` tries a few randomly selected entries. Qud's normal melee order can also be shuffled. The useful principle is **context-qualified, paid actions drawn from real capabilities**, with different behavior emerging from different equipment and bodies.

Evidence: [Qud candidate weights](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World/IAICommandListEvent.cs:38), [inventory-command dispatch and bounded random choice](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/AICommandList.cs:74), [offense/defense queries](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/Kill.cs:391), [shuffled melee priorities](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/Kill.cs:585).

CoO is already beyond “enemies only walk and hit.” It has finite self-medicine, weapon recovery, safe supported ability previews, preferred range, repositioning, water-then-electricity setup, local ally alerts, committed melee with recovery, and bounded last-seen pursuit. These should be extended rather than replaced. Evidence: [KillGoal decision order](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/Goals/KillGoal.cs:93), [owned ability checks](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/CombatTacticsPart.cs:113), [elemental setup and positioning](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/CombatTacticsPart.cs:220), [finite medicine](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/FieldMedicinePart.cs:97), [weapon recovery](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/WeaponRecoveryPart.cs:20), [visible commitment and recovery](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/CommittedMeleePart.cs:28).

## 1. Put two useful defensive item decisions into ordinary opponents

**Recommendation:** start with two authored, finite-kit behaviors: a burning equipped humanoid uses actual carried water or clay to put itself out; a retreating humanoid spends one actual Veilpuff to break a visible pursuer's sight. Scope the first milestone to two opponent loadouts at existing encounter destinations. Keep the rest of the bestiary unchanged. A repeatable neutral work demonstration can expose water/clay use before a hostile encounter does.

**Qud evidence:** defensive inventory commands are a real decision channel, not decorative equipment. Its healing tonic checks actual utility and applicability before offering a heavily weighted command. Its fire response can prefer a cooling liquid container and pour real liquid, with bounded attempts to find water. It also has retreat-specific ability candidates. [Healing utility gate](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.Parts/AIHealingTonicUse.cs:34), [real extinguishing container choice](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/ExtinguishSelf.cs:65), [paid pour and bounded pool search](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/ExtinguishSelf.cs:87), [retreat ability channel](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/Flee.cs:57). These are design references; do not reproduce Qud-specific objects or its code.

**CoO connection:** Kill/Flee explicitly invoke FieldMedicine, while its cure policy is deliberately restricted to Antidote/BurnSalve. The new water/clay actions already pay real supplies and remove/cool flames, and sight cover already interacts with bounded pursuit. In the inspected Kill/Flee paths, these new utility action families are not selected autonomously. This is a verified call-path gap, not a conclusion from a missing class name. [Medicine's exact cure list](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/FieldMedicinePart.cs:55), [Flee's current whole-action medicine decision](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/Goals/FleeGoal.cs:38), [native dousing/smothering choices and receipt](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/Items/EmergencyDousingActions.cs:18), [six-action lost-sight search](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/Goals/KillGoal.cs:65).

**Player scenario:** set a guard alight to interrupt pursuit. The guard spends its next opportunity dousing itself rather than swinging, consumes its finite water, and becomes wet: electricity now becomes a plausible follow-up. Later, a wounded scavenger uses cover and escapes; the player can circle the cover, wait out the search, or save the consumable by winning before it is used. The player learns the same tools they can loot.

**Cost and counterplay:** one NPC action, real inventory decrement, no instant heal/cast/move combination, no free refills, unused stock remains recoverable on death. Do not make every intelligent enemy perfectly prepared or universally counter every player build. Start with one emergency supply per role and a small retreat-cover allowance. Ordinary fire and electrical consequences apply to allies and enemies alike.

**Bounded implementation:** add an opt-in policy beside FieldMedicine, not a general planner or another resource counter. Reuse transactional execution, but first separate player menu visibility/messages from AI perception: equipment targets currently use player-visible cells and several feedback strings say “You.” Calling UI menus blindly would make offscreen AI behavior depend on the player's FOV. Use the NPC's existing LOS/knowledge checks and actor-aware visible feedback. Respect the scheduler's one-action ownership. [Player-facing utility seam](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/Items/EquipmentUtilityActions.cs:210), [feedback seam](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/Items/EquipmentUtilityActions.cs:220), [existing medicine scheduler contract](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/FieldMedicinePart.cs:91).

**Acceptance:** native whole-goal tests prove fire ends only after actual payment; an empty vessel leaves the normal action available; cover does not reveal a hidden player; the NPC cannot both consume and attack; used supplies disappear from death loot. One live neutral demonstration and one hostile encounter must show recognizable item/model motion and a brief visible message. Existing inspect hints should expose the carried kit and its finite state, following FieldMedicine's current stock description rather than adding a quest marker.

## 2. Make local movement respect the danger costs already implemented

**Recommendation:** apply the existing bounded, actor-specific terrain cost when choosing an ordinary approach step, retreat step, or firing position. Start with eight local candidate cells; request existing A* only when the direct approach has meaningful avoidable cost. Preserve a finite willingness to cross danger if there is no good route.

**Qud evidence:** a nonpanicked flee considers navigation weight and the combined difficulty of nearby hostiles, avoids recently visited cells, then has a fallback that maintains distance. Panic has a different tolerance. This is useful threat-sensitive selection, not proof that every Qud route is safe. [Flee local scoring](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/Flee.cs:110).

**CoO connection:** the danger model already respects gas, actual pools, slippery films, steam, body footprint and relevant resistance. A* uses it. However, the ordinary approach fast path takes a passable direct step before invoking weighted A*, TryStepAway only tries geometric directions, and ranged repositioning takes the first clear eligible shot cell. MovementSystem itself does not apply navigation danger costs. Therefore the gap is inconsistent use of an existing model, not absent hazard simulation. [Current cost model](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/TerrainNavigationWeight.cs:15), [A* cost consumer](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/FindPath.cs:179), [unweighted direct approach](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/AIHelpers.cs:414), [retreat step](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/AIHelpers.cs:457), [first clear firing position](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/CombatTacticsPart.cs:257).

**Player scenario:** scatter slippery oil across the short approach. A susceptible pursuer takes a visible side route, giving the player time to escape or aim. A creature immune to the actual gas still crosses its cloud. A cornered opponent may accept a bad tile rather than freeze forever. This makes placing supplies an influence on enemy movement, not just a random extra damage roll.

**Cost and limits:** smarter detours make direct kiting harder but reward intentional terrain work. Evaluate actual known/visible danger; do not reveal buried traps or infer unimplemented hazards from decorative tile names. Score all occupied contacts of large bodies. Avoid global zone scans, full combat rollouts, and perfect ally coordination. Keep authored reckless behavior possible.

**Acceptance:** pair identical layouts with/without real danger, immunity, grit and alternative routes; test the direct unobstructed chase as well as wall-blocked A*. Test one forced bad route, a large body's far foot, and no oscillation. Show a live opponent choosing a detour after the player's item placement. This is likely the highest systemic return per changed line.

## 3. Give one enemy role a displacement plan that teaches the new brace

**Recommendation:** add one supported displacement skill preview and one authored hostile kit, with an ordinary approach that players can recognize. Choose either Slam or Hook-and-Drag for the first milestone, not both. Give the actor a real matching weapon and make its weapon loss disable the tactic. Keep the first lesson on forgiving terrain; later encounters can put the same role near water, fire or a drop.

**Qud evidence:** Cudgel Slam only offers an AI command when adjacent, appropriately equipped, able to move its extremities, and off cooldown. Hook-and-Drag can offer a move-away decision after a hook is established, giving a skill a follow-up purpose instead of merely adding another damage button. [Slam capability gate](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.Parts.Skill/Cudgel_Slam.cs:58), [hook follow-up movement](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.Parts.Skill/Axe_HookAndDrag.cs:44). CoO's hook semantics differ: its pulls happen on the victim's turns. Preserve CoO's tested semantics rather than porting Qud's movement-trigger attacks.

**CoO connection:** the authored tactics factory supports eight explicit skills; these two are not among the supported previews. Their actual player mechanics already exist, including real displacement, collision damage/stun, saved Hooked, and the new one-force brace. Current committed melee already proves readable windup/recovery is possible, so this does not require a universal telegraph rewrite. [Supported skill set](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/CombatTacticsPart.cs:73), [Slam movement/brace/collision](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/Skills/Cudgel_Slam.cs:96), [existing hook application](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/Skills/Axe_HookAndDrag.cs:78), [brace rules and inspection](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/Items/ItemTacticalUseDescription.cs:59), [committed visible windup](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/CommittedMeleePart.cs:54).

**Player scenario:** an original hostile works enforcer visibly sets its weapon for a shove across a narrow dry lane. A player can sidestep, disarm it, use a snare to deny its approach, brace with boots, or brace beside a wall with wraps. Taking cover against a wall is not automatically best: without a brace, wall collision matters. A later version of the same encounter beside a wet channel makes prior knowledge useful.

**Cost and limits:** existing cooldown and equipment requirements; no hidden bonus reach; one action per step. Bracing stops displacement, not all damage or stun. Do not turn the neutral PeatCutter into an enemy: its current blueprint is a Villager with a carried dagger, not a cudgel fighter. Author the hostile role explicitly and source it through an actual encounter. [Neutral PeatCutter facts](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Resources/Content/Blueprints/Objects.json:33883). Existing marlback breachers are genuine authored armed opponents and illustrate how kits already spawn, but their present skill is Berserk and their weapon is an axe; do not silently pretend they already have Slam. [Breacher kit](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Resources/Content/Blueprints/Objects.json:18345), [live landmark source](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/World/Generation/Builders/LandmarkBuilder.cs:557).

**Acceptance:** first-impact and ally safety match real execution; invalid weapon/cooldown/control states fall back safely; braced/unbraced and dry/slippery countercases run through the native AI turn; disarmed enemy visibly changes behavior. The opening encounter must be survivable without knowing the trick and leave the actual usable gear behind. Discoverability is inferred from consistent demonstration plus real loot, not claimed as a directly copied Qud teaching system.

## 4. Preserve the player's escape choices during enemy retreat

**Recommendation:** extend CoO's observed-position discipline to retreat. Keep a short saved last-observed threat position for fleeing actors and evaluate locally seen threats when choosing escape direction. If a pursuer vanishes into cover, continue away from its last known approach for a bounded interval; do not continually steer away from its hidden live coordinates.

**Qud evidence and caution:** Qud's Flee reads the target's current cell, so copying that method would not deliver this fairness goal. Its ForceWall retreat provider separately selects threats through LOS, demonstrating that contextual defensive decisions can be perception-qualified. Use that principle and CoO's stronger existing pursuit policy. [Qud Flee live-target position](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.AI.GoalHandlers/Flee.cs:91), [LOS-qualified retreat defense](/Users/steven/qud-decompiled-project/XRL.World.Parts.Mutation/ForceWall.cs:50).

**CoO connection:** KillGoal records visible contacts and searches the last seen cell for six opportunities. FleeGoal instead reads FleeFrom's current position every action, then uses the geometric step-away helper; its general flee trigger is a health fraction. This is a specific inconsistency in the inspected branches, not a claim that all AI is omniscient. [Observed pursuit state](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/Goals/KillGoal.cs:65), [retreat live position read](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/Goals/FleeGoal.cs:45), [current health threshold](/Users/steven/caves-of-ooo/Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/AI/Goals/GoalHandler.cs:127).

**Player scenario:** a wounded raider flees south. The player makes cover, circles around and cuts off the known exit. The raider should not reverse simply because the hidden player moved behind a wall. Conversely, once it sees the player in the exit, it can pick another route, use its finite retreat supply, or fight when cornered.

**Cost and limits:** less omniscient retreat creates exploitable patterns; that is the intended reward for knowledge and preparation. Do not make fleeing actors forget obvious visible new threats or stand inert indefinitely. Preserve current finite medicine, whole-action scheduling and hostility. Bound state to one remembered threat, a few positions/turns and local visible candidates; no shared faction blackboard.

**Acceptance:** identical hidden-player movement must leave the same fleeing choice; visible reacquisition may change it; saved/reloaded memory must retain the same budget; cornered behavior remains live; NPCs cannot peek through the player's sight-cover residue. Pair this with recommendation 2's local danger scoring rather than maintaining two incompatible escape scorers.

## Suggested order and stopping point

First make movement costs consistent (#2), then ship one finite defensive kit (#1) and one displacement lesson (#3) in recognizable ordinary encounters. Add retreat memory (#4) alongside escape-cover AI if time permits. The completion target is a small set of fights where the player sees an enemy use a recoverable tool, learns a counter, and can reuse that knowledge elsewhere—not a large new ability count.

No recommendation needs a new broad simulation layer, copied enemies, a full utility-AI rewrite, universal combat overlays, or new world-map quest markers. Each should begin with a native failing scenario and end with a live ordinary-spawn demonstration; defer unrelated low-impact defects rather than let them consume the whole tranche.
