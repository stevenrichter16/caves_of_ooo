## Implemented checkpoint

The command owner integrates this API only after irreversible launch commitment:

```csharp
ThrowPayloadResolver.Resolve(actor, item, zone, trace.BurstCell,
    trace.HitEntity, trace.ImpactCell, focused, rng, actor.ID + ":" + launch.Serial);
```

The resolver snapshots unique current owners, rechecks actual footprint/contact membership immediately before each direct dose, and then deposits ground inputs once. Callback exceptions propagate to the command's committed-partial handler. The footprint clips sealed cells and double sealed corners; known-only previews skip hidden-owner geometry. A directly struck solid owner remains separate from its approach-side floor burst.

`LiquidPoolPart.Volume` is the sole residual matter counter. A finite film spends one unit before actual entry callbacks; dispatcher-owned pooled contact sets prevent a wide body taking the same launch/family twice. True later entries get a new dispatch. No extra initial contact dose occurs on newly placed film. The existing `LiquidCoveredEffect` supplies coating, drying and typed delayed damage. Optional source IDs retain same-source credit, clear mixed/unknown credit, and resolve only an unambiguous living actor in the current zone.

Each source owns its tile projection through `Layer.ProjectionOwnerId`. Removing or exhausting that source removes its lease only. Explicit coating consumption/clear retires the real matching finite source before reaction outputs, so freezing/boiling cannot leave a renewable water owner. Six active-zone player/world rounds age the source; `ResolveAfterAbility` and individual NPC turns do not. Layer accounting/readout deduplicate shared liquid names. Vessels, waterskins and drinking sources reject films; ordinary poured liquid is a separate pool rather than merging recoverable drams into exposure units.

Shared Zone relocation hooks notify `ThrowingRules` only after accepted graph mutations; these support actor/item preparation invalidation. Skills owner checked that current load reconstruction uses direct index rebuild rather than those relocation callbacks.

Observed tradeoffs, without changing the frozen tuning values:

- **Focused healing has a contextual benefit and an obvious cost.** In the2SP Focused package, the mixed ally/enemy layout's mean enemy HP change is+5.5 with Broad and−2.5 with Focused; ally HP changes−2.5 and−1.55 respectively after subsequent AI. In the two-allies layout Broad provides+13.95 combined ally HP, while Focused provides−0.05 over the same follow-up. One flask is spent in each policy. Buying Focused therefore does not replace Broad as the default for every situation.
- **Ground film influences routes without guaranteeing safety.** Broad acid causes a two-cell maximum vertical detour in the open lane and three in the bypass. Acid resistance100 removes that avoidance benefit. In the2SP Focused package, open-lane Focused ground loses15.55 mean player HP versus9.85 for Broad ground; concentration leaves a smaller footprint to route around. Direct AcidTonic is markedly better against the already visible, reachable target in these particular arenas. This is a countercondition to setting up the floor, not a reason to buff floor damage until it dominates ordinary direct use.
- **Existing controls remain competitive.** The available-control policy usually holds ordinary melee enemies away with less player damage, while spending a finite LightningTonic when its wet follow-up is legal. It retains the unused acid supply; it is not a zero-resource result. The ranged caster still deals mean10.35 player damage under that policy. No-spare and early-retreat results remain separate from successful resource use.
- **Order matters, but not every planned combination wins.** At0SP, Broad water then charge causes mean−12 enemy HP and0 player HP lost; charge then water causes−3 and5.35. The paid two-action sequence consumes two items either way. At2SP with Focused, water-first deals only−4 mean enemy HP, showing the lost coverage after the enemy's intervening movement. Acid-plus-cudgel and water-plus-cudgel orders sometimes tie; Driving and concentration do not magically force a favorable landing after the enemy gets a turn.
